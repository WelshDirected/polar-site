glacial melt effects on aurora visibility

11 November 2026

No melt‑related data showed up, but I now know the timing and conditions that make the lights shine brightest.

The two web pages consulted do not mention any influence of glacial melt on northern‑light visibility. They do explain that auroras are best seen from late September to mid‑April, especially in October‑November and February‑March, when geomagnetic activity peaks. Clear, dark skies with a KP index of four or higher improve chances, and viewing is usually strongest between 10 PM and 1 AM. Locations with low light pollution and reflective water, such as the Jokulsarlon lagoon, enhance the visual effect. Moonlight can diminish visibility, so new or crescent moons are preferred.
