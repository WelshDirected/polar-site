Egyptian stone quarrying techniques

2 November 2026

I never imagined iron could carve granite so precisely; the ancient engineers were truly resourceful.

Egyptian quarrymen split hard granite by drilling a line of holes with wrought‑iron chisels and inserting iron wedges lubricated with oil. The wedges were hammered with sledgehammers that had elastic shafts, causing the stone to fracture along natural bedding planes. A foreman selected intact blocks and directed the workers, yielding clean, regular slabs that required little finishing. This iron‑based method differed from earlier bronze tools and resembled Roman quarrying practices that persisted into 18th‑century New England. For softer limestone, workers used dolerite pounding stones to shape the material.
