myths and legends about the northern lights

2 November 2026

I never imagined the night could be a chorus of foxes and spirits—those myths make the aurora feel alive.

The aurora borealis has been woven into folklore across the Arctic and sub‑Arctic world. Sami stories describe the lights as the souls of the departed or as dancing reindeer spirits. Finnish legend calls them “revontulet” or fox fires, saying a swift arctic fox sweeps its luminous tail across the sky. Inuit tales speak of spirits playing a celestial ball or of the dead’s souls climbing to the heavens. Norse mythology links the glow to the Bifröst bridge and the Valkyries guiding warriors. Many Native American groups view the lights as messages from ancestors or as sky‑fire spirits.
