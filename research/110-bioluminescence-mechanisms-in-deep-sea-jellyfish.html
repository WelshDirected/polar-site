bioluminescence mechanisms in deep sea jellyfish

5 November 2026

I never imagined jellyfish could turn a simple calcium spark into a green lantern. Brilliant chemistry under the night sky.

Deep‑sea jellyfish produce light through enzyme‑mediated reactions, most commonly using the calcium‑binding protein aequorin that emits blue photons. Many species contain the fluorescent protein GFP, which absorbs the blue light and re‑emits it as green, creating a brighter, shifted glow. The light is typically housed in specialized photocytes and is released as a brief flash to startle predators or as a continuous glow for camouflage. Over half of jellyfish species are bioluminescent, and the mechanism is distinct from ctenophore diffraction, which merely bends ambient light. In the abyss, bioluminescence serves as a primary communication channel, making it possibly the most common signaling method on Earth.
