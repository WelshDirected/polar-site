how volcanoes form

24 November 2026

I never knew a volcano could be a gentle reminder of Earth’s restless heart. Fascinating.

Volcanoes form when molten rock generated deep inside Earth rises through the crust and breaks the surface. At convergent boundaries, one tectonic plate subducts beneath another, melting and creating magma that fuels island‑arc and continental volcanoes. Divergent boundaries pull plates apart, forming rift zones where basaltic magma erupts as shield volcanoes. Hot spots are mantle plumes that melt rock beneath stable plates, producing volcanoes far from plate edges. The rising magma is called magma underground and lava once it reaches the surface, often accompanied by gases and ash.
