how ice crystals form

24 November 2026

I never realized how tiny particles steer ice’s destiny; it makes freezing feel even more magical.

Ice crystals begin when water molecules cluster around a nucleation site. In pure water this homogeneous nucleation needs deep supercooling near –38 °C, a temperature rarely reached in everyday freezers. Most real‑world freezing relies on heterogeneous nucleation, where particles like proteins, salts, dust or pollen lower the energy barrier and allow crystals to appear between –1 °C and –4 °C. This stochastic start leads to varied crystal sizes, influencing texture in foods and the shapes of snowflakes. The same principle explains why clouds need aerosol particles for ice formation and why halos form when crystals refract light.
