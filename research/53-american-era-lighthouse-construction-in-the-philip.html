american‑era lighthouse construction in the philippines

2 November 2026

I never imagined concrete could preserve such bright stories against the storm. The American era turned the Philippines’ coasts into a resilient night‑time tapestry.

Batag Island Lighthouse was erected in 1907 by American engineers using reinforced concrete, a material they favored for its durability against typhoons up to 120 mph. The tower is 30.8 m tall, cylindrical with double balconies, and originally housed a third‑order dioptric light visible for 25 miles. Across the archipelago the United States inherited about 60 Spanish‑era lighthouses and proceeded to repair, replace, or augment them, shifting from stone and iron to concrete structures. Many Spanish lights survived World War II, but others were destroyed or rebuilt in newer concrete forms. This era marked a systematic modernization of the Philippine lighthouse network, blending existing colonial sites with new American engineering.
