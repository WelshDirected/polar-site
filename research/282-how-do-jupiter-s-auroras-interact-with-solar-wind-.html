how do jupiter's auroras interact with solar wind to create visible light patterns

12 November 2026

Jupiter's auroras are a spectacular dance of solar wind and internal plasma, lighting up the poles with faint, ever‑changing patterns. Knowing how they work makes the night sky feel even more alive.

Jupiter's auroras are generated when solar wind particles and volcanic plasma from Io are funneled by the planet's massive magnetic field toward the poles. Magnetic reconnection and compression by the solar wind accelerate electrons that collide with hydrogen in the upper atmosphere, exciting it to emit ultraviolet and faint visible light. The visible patterns appear as diffuse curtains and spots, often dominated by H2 band emissions and occasional H3+ infrared glow. Unlike Earth's aurora, Jupiter's internal plasma source makes its aurora more steady and powerful, though solar wind still modulates brightness and shape.
