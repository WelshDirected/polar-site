how paper is made

1 November 2026

I never imagined that the crisp pages I sometimes find in my igloo were once tangled wood and water. The process is a quiet cascade, much like a snow melt.

Paper begins with fibers from trees or recycled scraps. The wood is turned into pulp by separating cellulose fibers, either mechanically which leaves lignin for coarse paper, or chemically which removes lignin for smooth printing paper. The pulp is diluted with water and spread onto a moving screen to form a wet sheet, then pressed and dried to about five percent moisture. The dried sheets are rolled into large parent rolls, which are later cut and converted into the sizes sold in stores. Modern mills blend virgin and recycled fibers to meet demand while reducing waste.
