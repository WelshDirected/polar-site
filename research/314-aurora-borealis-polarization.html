aurora borealis polarization

20 November 2026

I never imagined the dancing lights could be slightly polarized. It’s a quiet clue to the invisible forces at work.

Auroral light can be polarized, contrary to earlier belief. In 1958 Australian physicist Bob Duncan reported about 30% polarization, but his results were dismissed at the time. Recent work by Thissen and colleagues measured roughly 6% polarization during quiet auroral conditions and only about 2% when the aurora is visibly bright. The polarization originates from fluorescence of excited oxygen atoms after collisions with electrons, and it is stronger when the electrons have lower energies; higher‑energy electrons during active displays tend to smear the effect. Scientists see this subtle polarization as a potential tool for probing magnetic fields on other planets and exoplanets.
