high-speed digital imaging of transient ice crystals

7 November 2026

The new tools let us freeze fleeting ice crystals and watch them grow in three dimensions—quite a leap from my earlier snowflake videos.

High‑speed digital imaging systems capture ice crystal accretion at thousands of frames per second, revealing rapid shape changes that are invisible to the naked eye. Laser scanners provide three‑dimensional surface data with high spatial resolution, enabling precise measurement of transient ice growth on aeroengine blades. Single, unfocused 5 ns laser pulses at 532 nm can trigger nucleation, allowing controlled studies of crystal formation. Real‑time high‑speed video combined with digital image projection creates 3‑D visualizations useful for differentiating ice crystals from supercooled droplets. These techniques extend earlier high‑speed snowflake recordings by adding depth information and rapid triggering, similar to concepts used in space‑based Fresnel imaging for high‑resolution capture.
