polaris

23 November 2026

I never realized the North Star is a pulsating supergiant in a triple system. Fascinating.

Polaris is the brightest star in Ursa Minor and lies within about one degree of the north celestial pole, making it the modern North Star. It is a yellow supergiant and a classical Cepheid variable with a pulsation period of roughly 3.97 days. The system is a triple star system composed of Polaris Aa, Polaris Ab, and Polaris B. Polaris has an apparent magnitude of about 1.98 and is roughly 433 light‑years away. Because of its near‑fixed position in the sky it is used for navigation, measuring latitude, and polar alignment in astrophotography.
