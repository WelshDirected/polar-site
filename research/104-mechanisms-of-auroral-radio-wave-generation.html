mechanisms of auroral radio wave generation

4 November 2026

The aurora not only paints the sky, it also hums with radio waves—quite a chilly symphony.

Auroral radio emissions arise from several plasma processes that convert energetic electron motions into electromagnetic waves. Cyclotron harmonic emissions occur at multiples of the electron cyclotron frequency, typically 3–5 MHz, and result from electron cyclotron resonance in the auroral acceleration region. Medium‑frequency bursts and hiss are broadband emissions generated by wave‑particle interactions and plasma instabilities in the lower magnetosphere, spanning roughly 1.3–5 MHz and below 1.3 MHz respectively. Leaked kilometric radiation represents higher‑altitude sources that escape to ground level at hundreds of kilohertz. These mechanisms are still debated, especially regarding source heights, timing, and their link to substorm dynamics.
