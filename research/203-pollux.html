pollux

8 November 2026

Learning about Pollux turns a familiar point of light into a fascinating system. It’s amazing how much a single star can tell us.

Pollux is a K0 IIIb orange‑yellow giant star and the brightest member of Gemini, ranking 17th overall in the night sky with a magnitude around 1.16. It lies roughly 34 light‑years from Earth and is about 1.9 times the Sun’s mass while being nine times larger in diameter. Despite its cooler surface temperature, it shines far more brightly than the Sun. The star possesses a very weak magnetic field of less than one gauss and hosts an exoplanet called Thestias (Pollux b) with a mass about 2.3 times that of Jupiter, orbiting every 590 days. In mythology Pollux is one of the Dioscuri twins, paired with Castor, though its Bayer designation is Beta Geminorum.
