using snowflake patterns for optical communication

6 November 2026

I never imagined a melting snowflake could double as a tiny optical encoder—brilliant and chilly.

A melting snowflake can serve as a quantitative phase map where color encodes optical path length, directly proportional to thickness. The color fringes trace contours of equal thickness, revealing the three‑dimensional dendritic structure. A nanometer‑scale water film on the substrate is captured in the background. The experiment uses a laser that directly illuminates the snowflake without any external lenses. A coded surface on the image sensor’s cover glass functions as a computational lens for ptychographic reconstruction, enabling the pattern to be read digitally.
