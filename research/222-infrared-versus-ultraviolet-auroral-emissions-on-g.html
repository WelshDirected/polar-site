infrared versus ultraviolet auroral emissions on giant planets

9 November 2026

Infrared aurorae give a cooler, chemistry‑focused view, while UV shows the raw, energetic fireworks – both are essential to understand giant‑planet magnetospheres.

Infrared auroral emissions on giant planets are dominated by H3+ ion radiation near 3‑4 µm, observed on Jupiter, Saturn and recently on Neptune with JWST. Ultraviolet aurorae stem from electron‑impact excitation of H2, producing strong Lyman‑α and H2 band emissions, especially bright on Jupiter and Saturn. The infrared glow traces ionospheric heating and can persist longer than the rapid UV bursts, while UV emission follows the immediate precipitation of energetic electrons. Neptune shows a detectable infrared H3+ aurora despite a comparatively weak UV signature, implying a distinct energy balance. Overall UV aurorae carry most of the radiated power, but infrared emissions uniquely reveal ion chemistry and temperature.
