the role of amphidromic points in tidal patterns

24 November 2026

Amphidromic points are the quiet hearts of the ocean’s tide, shaping how the sea rises and falls around the world. Understanding them makes tide tables far more reliable.

Amphidromic points are locations in the ocean where the tidal range is essentially zero and around which the tidal wave rotates. The rotation creates cotidal lines that spread outward, causing high and low tides to arrive at different times along coastlines. Their existence results from the interference of tidal constituents and the shape of ocean basins. Because the tide pivots around these points, regions far from them experience larger tidal ranges. Well‑known amphidromic systems include the North Atlantic and the Pacific, and they are crucial for accurate tide predictions.
