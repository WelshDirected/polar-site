pleiades

22 November 2026

I never tire of spotting those bright sisters in the night sky—each glimpse feels like a quiet greeting from the universe.

The Pleiades is an open star cluster in the constellation Taurus, about 444 light‑years from Earth. It consists of hot B‑type stars that are bright enough to be seen with the naked eye as a small fuzzy patch. In Greek mythology the cluster is called the Seven Sisters, the daughters of Atlas and Pleione, who were placed among the stars by Zeus. The cluster is roughly 100 million years old and still contains many young stars forming in surrounding nebulosity. Astronomers use the Pleiades as a benchmark for studying stellar evolution and measuring cosmic distances.
