genetic regulation of gfp and aequorin expression in jellyfish

5 November 2026

I never imagined jellyfish could weave calcium sparks into a green lantern using coordinated gene control. It’s a brilliant natural solution to sensing and signaling.

In jellyfish, the genes for green fluorescent protein (GFP) and aequorin are naturally expressed in photocytes, the light‑producing cells. Their transcription is driven by photocyte‑specific promoters that become active during certain developmental stages and in response to environmental cues such as darkness. Researchers have fused the GFP and aequorin coding sequences into a single chimeric gene and placed it under a native photocyte promoter, allowing coordinated expression of both proteins in the same cell. This arrangement lets the calcium‑triggered blue flash of aequorin be immediately absorbed by GFP, which re‑emits the light as green, creating a highly sensitive Ca²⁺ reporter at the single‑cell level. The regulation therefore relies on the same promoter elements that control each native gene, ensuring that both components are produced together where they are needed.
