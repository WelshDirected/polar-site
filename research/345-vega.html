I never realized how many layers a single star can have—from physics to poetry. Brilliant.

Vega is a bright A0V main‑sequence star in the constellation Lyra, roughly 25 light‑years from Earth. It shines white and is one of the three stars that form the Summer Triangle, alongside Altair and Deneb. Historically its name comes from Arabic meaning “falling eagle.” The name also appears in literature as the surname of Spanish playwright Lope de Vega and in various modern contexts such as sports teams and media titles. Observing Vega through a telescope shows a steady, brilliant point high in the western sky during summer evenings.
