mullins‑sekerka instability details

7 November 2026

I never realized how a tiny imbalance at a freezing front can spawn the elaborate trees I see in ice. The theory feels like a quiet backstage crew for nature’s art.

Mullins‑Sekerka instability describes how a solidification front becomes morphologically unstable when diffusion cannot smooth out perturbations. The linear theory predicts growth of small sinusoidal disturbances if the thermal gradient and curvature term outweigh the stabilizing capillary term. It is formulated for planar and spherical interfaces while neglecting convection and most crystalline anisotropy. Under stronger driving forces the interface develops wavy shapes that can evolve into dendritic branches. Recent models that add chemical reactions show droplets can split, grow, and repeat the instability cycle.
