how lunar tides affect earth's oceans

25 November 2026

I never imagined the Moon’s dance could sculpt the sea so precisely—its nightly verses make my fishing nights feel like a quiet concert.

The Moon's gravity is the primary driver of Earth's ocean tides. The Sun also pulls on the water, creating tides about half as strong as the lunar ones. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth line up at new or full moon, their forces combine to produce especially high and low spring tides. About a week later, when the Sun and Moon are at right angles to Earth, their pulls partially cancel, resulting in milder neap tides. Tidal strength varies with the Moon’s distance: perigee (closest) yields stronger tides, apogee (farthest) weaker. The Earth’s orbital distance from the Sun adds a seasonal tweak, with slightly stronger tides near perihelion in early January and weaker tides near aphelion in early July.
