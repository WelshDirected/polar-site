fresnel zone plates in astronomy

3 November 2026

Fresnel zone plates turn thin rings into powerful space telescopes, especially for high‑energy light. Their lightweight nature makes them a promising alternative for future astronomical missions.

Fresnel zone plates are diffractive optics that focus light by arranging alternating transparent and opaque rings so that light from each zone arrives in phase at a focal point. In astronomy they are attractive for X‑ray and extreme‑UV wavelengths because they can be made thin and lightweight, unlike traditional lenses or mirrors. Space concepts such as the Fresnel Imager propose a large, deployable zone plate paired with a detector spacecraft to achieve sub‑arcsecond resolution. Their main drawback is strong chromatic dispersion, so they work best over a narrow band or require corrective optics. Nevertheless, prototype missions have demonstrated imaging of solar flares and distant X‑ray sources using zone plates.
