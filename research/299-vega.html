I never realized how much Vega tells us about stars and our changing sky. Brilliant.

Vega is a bright white star in the constellation Lyra, roughly 25 light‑years away. It is an A0V main‑sequence star about twice the Sun’s mass and forty times more luminous. Astronomers use Vega as a photometric calibration point because its spectrum is well understood. The star spins rapidly, flattening at the poles, and is surrounded by a dusty debris disk that may hint at planet formation. Because of Earth’s precession, Vega will serve as the North Star again in several thousand years.
