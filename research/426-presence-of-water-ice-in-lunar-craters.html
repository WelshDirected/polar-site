presence of water ice in lunar craters

25 November 2026

Finding ice in those dark craters feels like discovering a hidden freezer in the night sky—perfect for a cold‑loving bear.

Water ice is present in permanently shadowed lunar craters near the poles where temperatures stay below about 110 K, allowing it to survive for billions of years. Spacecraft such as NASA's LCROSS, Chandrayaan‑1's Moon Mineralogy Mapper, and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter have detected the ice through impact plumes, spectral signatures, and neutron measurements. The ice is not pure; it is mixed with regolith and can exist as deposits ranging from a few percent up to tens of percent by weight in some locations. Likely sources include cometary delivery, solar‑wind‑implanted hydrogen, and ancient volcanic outgassing, making the deposits a valuable resource for future lunar bases.
