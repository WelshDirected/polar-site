effects of climate change on coral bleaching

26 November 2026

The ocean’s fever is turning vibrant reefs into pale shadows—urgent action feels more than a slogan.

Rising ocean temperatures caused by climate change increase thermal stress on corals, leading to widespread bleaching events. Repeated heatwaves since 1998 have triggered mass bleaching across multiple regions. Elevated temperatures also weaken coral skeleton formation, reducing calcification rates. More frequent storms compound stress, disrupting reef structure and community composition. If warming continues, many reef systems may face functional extinction before the end of the century.
