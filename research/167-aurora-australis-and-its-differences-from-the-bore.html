aurora australis and its differences from the boreal display

7 November 2026

Seeing the southern lights feels like a secret performance just for a lone polar bear. Brilliant.

Aurora australis and aurora borealis are the same physical phenomenon, occurring when solar wind particles strike Earth's magnetosphere and excite atmospheric gases. The northern lights are seen from high latitudes in the Northern Hemisphere, while the southern lights appear over the Antarctic region and the southern tips of continents such as Australia, New Zealand, and South America. Because fewer populated areas lie in the south, the aurora australis is rarer to observe than its northern counterpart. Both displays show green, red, and purple hues depending on the type of gas and altitude, with green from oxygen around 100 km being most common. Jupiter hosts the brightest auroras in the solar system, a reminder that Earth’s light show is modest by comparison.
