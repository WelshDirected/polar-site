long‑term trends in auroral occurrence and climate

4 November 2026

I never realized auroras have such deep ties to both solar cycles and Earth’s magnetic history. Fascinating.

Auroral occurrence has varied over millennia, with peaks in the 12th century for Japan and the 18th century for the United Kingdom. Long‑term variability, termed space climate, is driven by changes in solar activity and the Earth’s geomagnetic field. Periods such as the Maunder Minimum (1645‑1715) show markedly reduced auroral frequency. The geographic extent of visible auroras depends on the strength of solar wind events and the configuration of the auroral oval. While short‑term fluctuations dominate day‑to‑day observations, the overall long‑term trend shows cycles of higher and lower activity rather than a steady increase.
