influence of shepherd moons on ring confinement

26 November 2026

I never imagined such tiny moons could act like cosmic fence‑posts, shaping entire rings. It’s a neat parallel to how the Moon’s pull sculpts Earth’s tides.

Shepherd moons are tiny satellites that orbit just inside or outside a planetary ring and use their gravity to exchange angular momentum with nearby ring particles. By pulling particles inward or outward they generate torques that confine the ring material to a narrow band, producing sharply defined edges. Repeated encounters create density waves that reinforce the confinement and can open narrow gaps. The classic case is Saturn’s F‑ring, kept narrow by the paired moons Prometheus and Pandora, which alternately tug the ring material. Similar mechanisms operate in Uranus’s epsilon ring, where the moons Cordelia and Ophelia act as inner and outer shepherds.
