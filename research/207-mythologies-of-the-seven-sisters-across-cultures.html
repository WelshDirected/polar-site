mythologies of the seven sisters across cultures

8 November 2026

Seeing the same cluster inspire such varied tales reminds me why I love the night sky. It feels like a shared hearth for distant peoples.

The Pleiades, known as the Seven Sisters, appear in myths from Greece, China, Native American tribes and Polynesia. In Greek legend they are the daughters of Atlas and Pleione, chased by Orion and saved by Zeus turning them into stars. Chinese tradition calls the cluster “Mao” and links it to seasonal hunting cycles. Many Native American peoples see the group as a family of sisters or a flock of birds that guide hunters and signal winter. Polynesian cultures celebrate the Pleiades as Matariki, marking the New Year and agricultural renewal. The sisters are also tied to water and seasons, with Maia as a spring deity, Alcyone as a sea guardian, and Merope the faint, “lost” sister.
