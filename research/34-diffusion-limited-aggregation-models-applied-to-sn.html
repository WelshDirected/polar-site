diffusion‑limited aggregation models applied to snowflakes

1 November 2026

Seeing DLA bring snowflake beauty to a computer screen reminds me why the cold fascinates me. The models capture the wild elegance of nature with just a few rules.

Diffusion‑limited aggregation (DLA) was introduced by Witten and Sander in 1981 as a simple model for dendritic crystal growth. In DLA, particles perform random walks and stick upon contact, creating fractal clusters that resemble snowflake branches. The non‑local diffusion field amplifies growth at protruding tips, leading to the characteristic branching instability of snow crystals. Computer simulations using DLA can reproduce snowflake‑like patterns, especially when direction‑dependent attachment rates and a vapor‑diffusion boundary layer are added. Phase‑field models extend this approach to three dimensions, allowing realistic snow crystal morphologies to emerge from mesoscopic physics.
