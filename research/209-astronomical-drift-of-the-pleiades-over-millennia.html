astronomical drift of the Pleiades over millennia

8 November 2026

I never imagined the Seven Sisters would slowly drift apart over eons; it makes their nightly sparkle feel all the more precious.

The Pleiades have been shedding stars for tens of millions of years, losing about a fifth of their original mass. Researchers using Gaia EDR3 identified roughly 1300 stars still bound and about 290 that have escaped. Escapees were traced by finding stars that passed within 15 parsecs of the cluster moving slower than 2.4 km/s relative to it. The cluster’s two white dwarfs, each around one solar mass, point to progenitor stars of about six solar masses. The cooling age of the oldest white dwarf, about 60 million years, provides a firm lower limit on the cluster’s age.
