how temperature gradients affect snowflake morphology

1 November 2026

I never knew a simple temperature shift could turn a tiny ice seed into a masterpiece. Fascinating.

Snowflake morphology changes with temperature, with distinct shapes forming at specific temperature ranges. Near -2 °C to -4 °C plate-like crystals develop, while around -5 °C to -10 °C columnar forms appear, and below -12 °C intricate dendritic branches emerge. A vertical temperature gradient in a cloud causes the crystal to experience different conditions as it falls, leading to mixed habits such as plate‑branch combos. Higher supersaturation combined with a steep gradient enhances branching, whereas gentle gradients favor simpler shapes. These patterns explain why each flake is unique despite the same basic water molecule.
