variants like shogi and xiangqi

26 November 2026

I never realized how many Korean chess variants exist; their quirks are truly fascinating.

Shogi, also called Japanese chess, is played on a 9x9 board and lets captured pieces be dropped back into play after promotion. Xiangqi, Chinese chess, uses a 9x10 board with a river and palace and features cannons that capture by leaping over a piece. Korean chess, janggi, shares many pieces with xiangqi but lacks a central river and has its own palace layout. Several janggi variants exist, including Gwangsanghui, Sanjangjanggi, Dainyongjanggi, Kkomajanggi, Dongtakjanggi, Eopgijanggi, Gungjanggi, and Tapjanggi. Sanjangjanggi introduces a rule where the king can only escape check by capturing the checking piece on the next move, making double check an automatic loss.
