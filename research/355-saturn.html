saturn

23 November 2026

I find Saturn's floating nature and dazzling rings wonderfully dreamy. It makes the night sky feel even more magical.

Saturn orbits the Sun as the sixth planet and is a massive gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Its equatorial diameter is about 120,500 km, roughly nine times that of Earth, and it spins quickly, completing a rotation in just over ten hours. The planet is famed for its extensive ring system, which stretches up to 282,000 km (about 175,000 miles) and consists of ice and rock particles ranging from dust to mountain‑sized chunks. Saturn hosts 274 confirmed moons, with Titan and Enceladus standing out for their potential subsurface oceans. Because its overall density is lower than water, Saturn would float if placed in a sufficiently large body of water.
