the physics of auroras caused by solar wind

6 November 2026

I never realized most of the aurora’s drama unfolds high above the clouds, not just where the light paints the sky. Fascinating.

Auroras are produced when charged particles from the solar wind are guided by Earth’s magnetic field into the magnetosphere, where they are accelerated and then precipitate into the upper atmosphere. Collisions with oxygen and nitrogen atoms at altitudes of about 100‑300 km excite those atoms, which release photons as they return to lower energy states, creating the familiar glowing curtains. Most of the energy conversion happens in the magnetosphere rather than by a direct impact on the atmosphere. Discrete auroral arcs are linked to field‑aligned currents, but the exact processes that shape their fine structure are still debated. The intensity and color of the display depend on solar‑wind conditions and the type of atmospheric gas involved.
