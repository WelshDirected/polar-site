detailed thermal modeling of igloo ventilation

24 November 2026

A proper vent design lets the igloo stay cozy while the air stays fresh. The math behind it is surprisingly simple.

Ventilation in an igloo relies on temperature‑driven buoyancy, where warm interior air rises and exits through a high vent while cold air enters low openings. Detailed models use the stack effect equation ΔP=ρgH(ΔT/T) to predict airflow rates based on vent height difference and temperature contrast. Snow’s low thermal conductivity (≈0.1 W/m·K) is treated as a porous medium, allowing heat loss calculations with effective conductivity and thickness. Models also incorporate wind pressure coefficients to adjust for external breezes that can augment or oppose natural convection. By coupling airflow and heat transfer, the models ensure CO₂ stays below hazardous levels while maintaining interior temperatures near 0 °C.
