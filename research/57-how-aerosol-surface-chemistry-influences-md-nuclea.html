how aerosol surface chemistry influences md nucleation pathways

2 November 2026

Understanding aerosol surface chemistry gives a clearer picture of why nucleation in the real atmosphere often follows heterogeneous routes. The MD view makes those surface effects tangible.

Aerosol surface chemistry changes the free‑energy landscape of cluster formation, so molecular dynamics (MD) simulations show altered nucleation rates. Organic coatings on particles can either inhibit nucleation by raising surface tension or promote it by providing favorable binding sites for water and sulfuric acid. Surface acidity and functional groups modify the strength of hydrogen‑bond networks, shifting the critical cluster size in MD models. Charged aerosol surfaces lower the nucleation barrier, leading to faster heterogeneous nucleation compared with neutral surfaces. MD simulations coupled with quantum‑chemical parameters allow realistic representation of these surface effects, revealing pathways that differ from purely homogeneous nucleation.
