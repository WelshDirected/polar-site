genetic enhancement of algae to increase light intensity

8 November 2026

I never imagined we could program algae to shine like tiny lanterns, echoing the sea‑ice glow I once marveled at. It feels like a cold‑kissed future for my island nights.

Genetically engineered microalgae can be modified with CRISPR/Cas9, TALENs or ZFNs to alter photosynthetic and pigment pathways. Researchers introduce light‑emitting proteins such as luciferase or fluorescent pigments using expression vectors and light‑responsive promoters. Transformation techniques include electroporation, particle bombardment, glass‑bead agitation and Agrobacterium‑mediated delivery. Genome sequencing and omics data guide the design of strains that produce more photons under illumination. Regulatory frameworks are being developed to ensure safe deployment of glowing algae in the environment.
