the history of the polar bear in Inuit folklore

5 November 2026

I never imagined the polar bear could be both a fierce hunter and a wise teacher in Inuit tales. It feels warm even on my icy island.

Polar bears appear in Inuit oral tradition as powerful spirit beings linked to the sea and hunting. They are often portrayed as shape‑shifters, sometimes turning into humans or other animals in stories. In many legends the bear is a guardian of the Arctic, teaching respect for the ice and the hunt. Some myths describe the bear as a creator figure who helped shape the land and taught people how to survive. The name Nanook, meaning 'polar bear', is used for both the animal and a cultural hero who brings food and guidance.
