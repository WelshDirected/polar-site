low‑light noise reduction algorithms

10 November 2026

I appreciate how a light touch lets the night sky keep its natural texture while still taming the harshest speckles.

Low‑light noise reduction algorithms aim to suppress the grain and colour speckles that appear when cameras boost ISO for dim scenes. They usually separate luminance and colour components and apply spatial or frequency‑domain filters such as wavelet denoising or deep‑learning models. Lightroom and Photoshop expose separate luma and colour sliders, allowing users to keep grain visible by using low values (e.g., overall strength 15‑25). Heavy reduction can smooth fine detail and produce a plasticky look, so many photographers prefer minimal settings, especially in astrophotography where image stacking already reduces noise. New AI‑based tools claim better edge preservation but still risk over‑smoothing if set too high.
