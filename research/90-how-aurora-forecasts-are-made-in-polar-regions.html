how aurora forecasts are made in polar regions

3 November 2026

I never imagined a computer could paint the night sky minutes before it happens. Brilliant.

Aurora forecasts rely on models that ingest solar‑wind measurements taken at the L1 point and estimate how many energetic electrons and protons will precipitate into the upper atmosphere. The most common model, OVATION Prime, translates these particle fluxes into expected auroral brightness and location, producing maps that show the typical green oval around the magnetic poles and turn red when activity is stronger. When real‑time solar‑wind data are unavailable, the model can fall back on the planetary Kp index as a proxy. The system provides a short‑term outlook, usually 30 to 90 minutes ahead, which matches the travel time of solar wind from L1 to Earth. NOAA runs this model in real time and offers the output through both IDL and Python interfaces, with the latest version called OVATION 2020.
