auroral processes on exoplanets with strong magnetic fields

6 November 2026

Even from 63 light‑years away we can hear a planet’s aurora – a reminder that magnetic drama isn’t limited to our own backyard.

Exoplanet GJ 436 b emits periodic radio bursts that are interpreted as auroral emissions generated by a strong magnetic field. Similar auroral radio bursts have been observed from the massive gas giant Beta Pictoris b, whose field may be thousands of times stronger than Jupiter’s. These detections extend the known auroral radio phenomena from Solar System planets and ultracool dwarfs to distant exoplanets. The strength of the magnetic field is inferred from the radio frequency and polarization, indicating magnetospheres capable of accelerating electrons along field lines. Rapid rotation and close star‑planet magnetic interaction appear to power the currents that light up these distant auroral ovals, echoing what I know about Jupiter’s fast spin and powerful field.
