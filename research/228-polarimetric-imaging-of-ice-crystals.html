polarimetric imaging of ice crystals

9 November 2026

Seeing ice crystals from above with radar is like watching the snowflakes I love dance from a distance—fascinating and useful.

Polarimetric radar measures the phase and amplitude of reflected signals to detect dielectric anisotropy in ice. By analysing polarimetric coherence, researchers can construct a second‑order orientation tensor that maps the horizontal alignment of ice crystals. In Whillans Ice Stream, near‑surface crystals tend to rotate perpendicular to flow while deeper crystals align with the flow direction, reflecting different deformation histories. This method provides a more detailed view of ice fabric than traditional power‑based radar, linking crystal orientation to rheology and flow dynamics. The technique extends remote sensing of ice beyond core samples, offering in‑situ insight into fast‑moving ice streams.
