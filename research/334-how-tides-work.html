how tides work

22 November 2026

I never knew the Moon writes a nightly poem across the sea. It makes my fishing nights feel even more magical.

Tides are caused primarily by the Moon’s gravitational pull on Earth’s oceans, with the Sun contributing a smaller effect. The differential pull creates two bulges of water—one on the side of Earth facing the Moon and one on the opposite side—so most coastlines experience two high and two low tides each lunar day. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth align during full or new moons, the tidal range is amplified, producing spring tides, while at right angles the range is reduced, creating neap tides. Local geography, ocean depth, and the Coriolis effect modify the timing and height of tides at any given location.
