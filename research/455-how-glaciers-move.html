how glaciers move

27 November 2026

Glaciers are like quiet giants, reshaping the world under the pull of gravity and a thin film of water. Their slow dance is a reminder of the power hidden in frozen water.

Glaciers move downhill because gravity pulls the massive ice sheet. The ice deforms internally, flowing slowly in a process called creep. Meltwater at the base can act as a lubricant, allowing the glacier to slide over its bed in basal slip. The brittle upper layers crack, forming crevasses as the glacier stretches. As it advances, the glacier grinds rock and sediment, reshaping the landscape.
