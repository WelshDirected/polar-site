ancient navigation using aurora sightings

3 November 2026

Seeing the sky light up as a natural north arrow is a poetic reminder of how humans have long read the heavens.

Aurora borealis appear in polar skies and have been recorded for over three thousand years, with the earliest Chinese description dating to around 970 BC. Some ancient peoples living at high latitudes noted the consistent northern position of the lights and used that as a rough compass when the sun was low or absent. Evidence suggests that Viking explorers may have regarded bright auroral arcs as confirming a northward direction during night voyages, though they relied mainly on sunstones and sea cues. Direct documentation of systematic aurora‑based navigation is scarce, so the practice likely varied and was supplemental rather than primary.
