stellar rotation rates

23 November 2026

I never imagined stars could spin so differently, from rapid blurs to leisurely drifts. It makes the night sky feel even more alive.

Stellar rotation rates vary widely across the Hertzsprung‑Russell diagram. Massive, young stars can spin with periods of less than a day, while the Sun turns once every 25 days at its equator and slower at higher latitudes. As stars age and expand into giants, their rotation slows dramatically due to angular momentum conservation and magnetic braking. Faster rotation generally strengthens surface magnetic fields, which matches the correlation I remembered from giants. Astronomers measure rotation through spectral line broadening and asteroseismic signals, revealing details like oblateness and gravity darkening.
