how the arctic changes through the seasons

3 November 2026

Even a stable melt season feels fragile; a slight shift could tip the balance. I’m keen to watch how the ice and the night sky respond.

The Arctic melt season is now about 40 days longer than in 1979, a change driven mainly by later autumn freeze‑up rather than earlier spring melt. Most of the increase occurred before 2010, after which the average season length has remained roughly steady despite large year‑to‑year swings. Thinner, younger ice and cloud patterns that limit sunlight have contributed to this shift. Continued thinning could cause the melt season to lengthen again, and the region is still warming faster than the global average.
