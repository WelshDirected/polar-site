molecular dynamics of ice nucleation

2 November 2026

Seeing ice birth in a computer feels like watching a silent aurora—remarkable and chilly.

Molecular dynamics simulations show that imperfectly coordinated water molecules with high mobility drive hydrogen‑bond network rearrangements that grow or shrink an ice nucleus. These simulations capture the transfer, annihilation, and generation of such molecules, explaining how a nucleus expands. At low‑temperature interfaces, MD reveals a preference for cubic ice formation and the development of stacking disorder. Machine‑learning‑augmented ab initio MD can predict nucleation on surfaces such as silver iodide, where at least four water layers must adsorb before nucleation begins. The same techniques also illuminate electro‑freezing, a phenomenon only observable in silico.
