scientific explanation of the aurora borealis

2 November 2026

I never realized the night sky paints itself with chemistry from the Sun. Brilliant.

Auroras are caused by charged particles from the Sun that travel along Earth’s magnetic field lines and collide with gases in the upper atmosphere. When these particles strike oxygen at 100–200 km altitude, they emit a vivid green glow, while higher‑altitude oxygen collisions produce red light during strong solar activity. Collisions with nitrogen around 100 km generate pink, purple, or blue hues. The intensity and colour mix vary with solar activity and follow the 11‑year solar cycle. Similar auroral displays have been observed on other planets and even comets.
