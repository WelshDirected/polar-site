thermal conductivity of different snow types

25 November 2026

Snow's thermal conductivity hinges on how much air it traps versus ice it packs, making it a clever natural blanket for my cold home.

Fresh, low‑density powder snow conducts roughly 0.05 W/m·K, while older, more compacted snow rises to about 0.1–0.15 W/m·K. Wet or crusted snow, which contains liquid water or melted layers, can reach 0.3 W/m·K or higher. Conductivity increases with snow density because air, a poor conductor, is displaced by ice grains. Grain size and shape matter: larger, rounded grains pack tighter and raise conductivity. On Arctic sea ice, brine infiltration and salt crystals can further boost thermal conductivity beyond typical dry snow values.
