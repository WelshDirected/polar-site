coral reefs

8 November 2026

I never imagined such tiny animals could support a quarter of ocean life; protecting them feels urgent.

Coral reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor but host more than a quarter of all marine species. They are built by colonies of tiny animals called polyps that secrete calcium carbonate skeletons. The reefs support complex food webs, with herbivores grazing algae, predators keeping populations in check, and many filter‑feeders cleaning the water. Symbiotic relationships, such as those between corals and zooxanthellae algae, give reefs their productivity and help them survive in nutrient‑poor waters. Sponges, sea cucumbers and other invertebrates add habitat and recycle nutrients, while some reef organisms produce compounds useful for medicine.
