interaction of humidity and temperature on snowflake shapes

1 November 2026

The dance of temperature and moisture sculpts every snowflake uniquely. Knowing the rules makes the glittering sky feel even more intimate.

Snowflake shapes depend strongly on the temperature at which the crystal grows and the surrounding water‑vapor supersaturation. Between about ‑2 °C and ‑5 °C low humidity produces simple plate‑like crystals, while high humidity yields ornate stellar plates. Around ‑15 °C high supersaturation drives the classic dendritic branches that sparkle in the night sky, whereas low humidity favors small, compact plates. Near ‑30 °C the crystal tends to form columnar or needle‑like shapes, again becoming more branched when humidity is high. Small shifts in either temperature or humidity can move a crystal from one form to another, illustrating how delicate the balance is between the two variables.
