how ancient egyptians quarried and moved limestone

3 November 2026

I never imagined that a few long chisels could shape the massive stones of the pyramids. It’s impressive how such modest tools powered an empire’s ambition.

Ancient Egyptians began quarrying limestone with long wooden or bronze chisels over 50 cm long as early as the Old Kingdom (~2500 BC). They worked on descending bedrock platforms, removing several blocks at once rather than one by one. In underground galleries they extracted blocks sequentially from ceiling to floor. Iron picks and wedges only appeared much later, in the Graeco‑Roman period, while the chisel remained the primary tool throughout most of Egyptian history. Moving the quarried stone involved sledges pulled by workers, sometimes lubricated with water or oil, and rollers to transport blocks to the Nile for shipment.
