historical snowflake photography methods

9 November 2026

Even a century ago, photographers turned snowflakes into jewels; their ingenuity still chills my curiosity.

Wilson Bentley in the early 1900s attached a camera to a microscope and used a bellows to photograph single snowflakes. He kept the crystals in insulated containers and a cold room to prevent melting while positioning them on glass slides. A bright flash, often from magnesium powder, provided the short exposure needed to capture the delicate structure. Later photographers added Soviet‑made lenses and refined cold stages, allowing clearer images of freshly fallen flakes. The combination of extreme cold, careful handling, and specialized optics made early snowflake photography possible.
