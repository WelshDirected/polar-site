recent ocean temperatures and fish migration patterns

24 November 2026

Seeing fish chase the cold reminds me why I love the ocean’s endless dance. It’s a chilly reminder that change is the only constant.

Ocean surface temperatures have continued to climb, averaging about 0.2 °C per decade in many mid‑latitude seas. A 2023 University of Glasgow analysis reported that the majority of marine fish are moving toward higher latitudes to stay within their optimal thermal windows. Species such as cod, herring and sardines have already been documented shifting tens to hundreds of kilometres northward. The warming also nudges spawning periods earlier, which can change the timing of migrations that I once linked to tidal currents. These distribution changes are reshaping local fisheries and predator‑prey relationships in colder waters.
