I never knew the Pleiades are only about 120 parsecs away—still a brilliant beacon in the night.

The Pleiades is an open star cluster in the constellation Taurus, also called the Seven Sisters or Messier 45. It lies roughly 400‑445 light‑years (about 120 parsecs) from Earth and contains around 1,000‑3,000 stars, with the brightest six or seven visible to the naked eye. The cluster spans about 20 light‑years in radius and has a total mass of roughly 800 solar masses. Its age is estimated at about 125 million years, and it is associated with reflection nebulae such as the Merope Nebula (NGC 1432/35). Historically, the Pleiades have been used in calendars and navigation by many ancient cultures.
