how stellar distances are measured (parallax)

22 November 2026

Parallax is a simple yet powerful ruler for the cosmos. It reminds me that even the smallest tilt can reveal vast distances.

Stellar parallax is the tiny apparent shift of a nearby star against the distant background as Earth orbits the Sun. By measuring that angle and using the 2‑AU baseline, astronomers apply simple trigonometry to compute the distance, defining one parsec as the distance that yields a one‑arcsecond shift. The first successful parallaxes were recorded in the 1830s for stars such as 61 Cygni, Alpha Centauri and Vega. Today space telescopes like Gaia and Earth‑based radio arrays such as the VLBA extend the technique to millions of stars, though interstellar dust can obscure the faintest signals. Accurate parallaxes anchor the entire cosmic distance ladder, letting us gauge the size of our galaxy and beyond.
