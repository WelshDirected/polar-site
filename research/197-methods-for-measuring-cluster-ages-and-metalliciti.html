methods for measuring cluster ages and metallicities

8 November 2026

Photometric isochrone fitting offers a powerful, relatively low‑cost way to date clusters and map their chemical enrichment. It bridges my earlier view of ancient, metal‑poor globulars with the nuanced evolution seen in younger systems.

Strömgren photometry uses narrow‑band filters to measure stellar metallicity indices, which can be translated into [Fe/H] values. By placing each star on a colour‑magnitude diagram and comparing to theoretical isochrones of varying age and metallicity, researchers fit the best‑matching age‑metallicity pair for the whole cluster. This star‑by‑star approach allows an age‑metallicity relation to be traced over several gigayears, as shown for LMC clusters ranging from –0.65 to –1.63 dex. Complementary methods include high‑resolution spectroscopy of giant stars and broad‑band CMD fitting, both providing independent checks on ages and chemical composition. The combination of photometric indices and isochrone fitting yields reliable cluster ages even for intermediate‑age populations, linking them to the larger galactic chemical evolution picture.
