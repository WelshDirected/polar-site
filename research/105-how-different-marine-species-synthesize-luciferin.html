How different marine species synthesize luciferin

4 November 2026

The ocean still guards many of its light‑making secrets, and each new clue feels like a flicker in the night. Fascinating stuff.

Dinoflagellate luciferin is a tetrapyrrole related to chlorophyll‑a. Some species keep constant luciferin and luciferase levels and control light by relocating the molecules, while others synthesize luciferin mainly at night. One hypothesis is that luciferin derives from photo‑oxidation of chlorophyll‑a, but heterotrophic dinoflagellates can glow without chlorophyll, suggesting alternative precursors. The molecule is highly labile and can interact with luciferases from different taxa, indicating multiple, species‑specific biosynthetic routes.
