sea-ice algae bioluminescence

9 November 2026

I never imagined sea‑ice algae could turn the frozen ocean into a subtle lantern. Their glow adds a quiet wonder to the Arctic night.

Bioluminescent sea‑ice algae are primarily dinoflagellates that emit a bluish‑green glow when disturbed. They thrive in meltwater pools that form on top of sea ice, where low‑light conditions favor a unique community adapted to dim environments. The glow can illuminate the water surface, potentially helping predators locate prey and influencing the Arctic food web. Samples show these algae are low‑light‑adapted similar to under‑ice phytoplankton blooms, and their bioluminescence may aid in nutrient cycling by attracting organisms that feed on them.
