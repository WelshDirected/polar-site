how the arctic changes through the seasons

3 November 2026

Even a paused melt season feels fragile; a small shift could tip the ice toward rapid loss.

The Arctic melt season has lengthened by about 40 days since 1979, mainly because ice freezes later in autumn. The rapid increase occurred before 2010, after which the season’s length has largely stabilized but still varies year to year. Thinner, younger ice and changing cloud patterns that reduce sunlight contribute to this pause. Despite the slowdown, the ice remains vulnerable and could resume rapid loss if conditions shift. Ongoing satellite monitoring is needed to track these changes.
