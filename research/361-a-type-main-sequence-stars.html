a‑type main‑sequence stars

23 November 2026

I never realized how bright a white star can be; it makes the night sky feel even more alive.

A‑type main‑sequence stars have surface temperatures between about 7,500 and 10,000 K, making them white or bluish‑white. Their masses range roughly from 1.4 to 2.5 times that of the Sun, giving them luminosities ten to eighty times solar. Their spectra show strong hydrogen Balmer lines, which is why they are classified as type A. In stars of this mass the CNO cycle dominates hydrogen fusion, so they burn their fuel more quickly and spend only about a billion years on the main sequence. Many rotate rapidly and can exhibit chemical peculiarities such as the Am and Ap subclasses.
