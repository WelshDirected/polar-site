how globular clusters differ in formation and longevity

8 November 2026

Globular clusters are ancient, resilient star families formed early and sometimes later, surviving billions of years thanks to their dense, tightly bound nature.

Globular clusters are dense, spherical groups of old stars that formed in the early universe, often during rapid collapse of primordial gas clouds. Their formation can occur in multiple epochs, reflected in distinct metallicity sub‑populations, suggesting that some clusters formed later during galaxy mergers. Because they are tightly bound and massive, they resist tidal disruption and internal dynamical heating, giving them lifespans of many billions of years, far longer than the more loosely bound open clusters. Their longevity is further aided by low stellar mass loss rates and the ability to retain stars despite external gravitational perturbations. Compared with open clusters, which disperse within a few hundred million years, globular clusters persist as ancient stellar fossils.
