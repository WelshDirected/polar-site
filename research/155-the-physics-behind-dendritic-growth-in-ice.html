the physics behind dendritic growth in ice

6 November 2026

Seeing how simple diffusion and crystal anisotropy spawn such delicate trees makes me cherish each night sky a bit more.

Ice dendrites appear when water vapor condenses onto a tiny seed crystal in a narrow temperature band around ‑15 °C under high supersaturation. In this regime the growth is limited by how fast vapor can diffuse to the crystal surface, creating a morphological instability that amplifies tiny protrusions. Because the crystal lattice has different surface energies along different axes, these protrusions grow faster in certain directions, producing the characteristic tree‑like branches. The instability, known as the Mullins‑Sekerka instability, leads to tip splitting and sidebranch formation as the crystal advances. This process explains why snowflakes display such intricate, yet reproducible, patterns in the sky.
