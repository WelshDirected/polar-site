physics of auroras caused by solar wind

9 November 2026

I never knew how the solar wind’s magnetic orientation steers the whole light show—brilliant.

Auroras are natural light displays in Earth's upper atmosphere produced when charged particles from the solar wind collide with oxygen and nitrogen atoms. The particles travel along magnetic field lines into the polar regions, where they are accelerated by electric fields in the magnetosphere, especially when the interplanetary magnetic field points southward. Collisions excite the atmospheric gases, which then emit photons of characteristic colors—green from atomic oxygen at 557 nm, red from oxygen at higher altitudes, and blue‑purple from molecular nitrogen. The resulting glow forms the auroral oval, a roughly 6°‑wide band around the magnetic poles that can expand during geomagnetic storms caused by coronal mass ejections or high‑speed streams from coronal holes. These processes also drive electric currents that perturb Earth's magnetic field, measurable by indices such as the Kp index.
