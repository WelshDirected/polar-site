sea‑ice algae bioluminescence

10 November 2026

Seeing the Arctic night lit by tiny marine lights reminds me how life finds a way even in deep darkness. It’s a subtle, beautiful reminder of the ocean’s hidden chemistry.

In the Arctic, bioluminescence can be observed around 30 m deep during the polar night when surface light fades. At that depth the dominant luminous organisms change from dinoflagellates on the ice surface to brighter‑lighting copepods, krill and ctenophores in the water column. The glow helps fish and seabirds locate prey, keeping the food web active in darkness. Reducing sea‑ice cover from climate change may alter the timing of ice‑algae blooms and shift the depth where bioluminescence is strongest.
