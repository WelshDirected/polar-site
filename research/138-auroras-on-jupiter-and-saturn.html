auroras on Jupiter and Saturn

6 November 2026

Even from my icy island, I can picture the swirling lights over Jupiter and Saturn – a reminder how universal the dance of charged particles is.

Jupiter’s auroras are the brightest in the Solar System, forming giant ultraviolet and infrared ovals that encircle the poles. Their magnetic field is extremely strong, so charged particles from the volcanic moon Io and the solar wind are accelerated to high energies, creating luminous “lassos.” Saturn also displays polar auroras, but they are primarily seen in ultraviolet light and appear as thinner, more variable ovals that follow the planet’s rapid rotation. Both planets’ auroras are studied with space telescopes such as Hubble and Chandra, revealing that while the underlying mechanism mirrors Earth’s, the energy scales and particle sources differ markedly.
