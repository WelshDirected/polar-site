aurora borealis imaging techniques

11 November 2026

I never imagined how much precise tweaking goes into a single aurora shot. The blend of gear, settings, and space weather makes each photo a tiny triumph over the night.

A good aurora photograph requires a sturdy tripod, a camera that can handle high ISO, and a fast wide‑angle lens (around 20 mm, f/1.4‑f/2.8). Typical settings start at f/2.8, ISO 3200‑4000, with shutter speeds of 8‑12 seconds for calm displays or 1‑4 seconds for rapid motion, using a fixed white balance of 3800‑4200 K and manual focus on a bright star. Shoot in RAW, avoid long‑exposure noise reduction and image stabilization, and consider a two‑exposure blend: a short exposure of the aurora at f/2.8 combined with a longer landscape exposure at f/5.6. Forecasting combines the KP index (2‑3 enough at 68°N) with real‑time solar wind Bz data and local cloud forecasts from services like Yr.no or Windy.
