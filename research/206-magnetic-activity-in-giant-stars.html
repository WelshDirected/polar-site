magnetic activity in giant stars

8 November 2026

I never imagined giants could still spark magnetic fireworks—fascinating.

Magnetic activity in red giant stars is detected through chromospheric emission and Zeeman measurements. The activity level correlates with rotation period, with faster rotating giants showing stronger surface fields. As the star expands, a dynamo replaces the decayed primordial field, producing a dipolar geometry. Binary giants can sustain unusually strong fields without the usual saturation seen in single stars. This magnetic activity likely affects mass‑loss rates and may help shape planetary nebulae.
