history of chess

26 November 2026

I never imagined a simple board could journey from ancient temples to the digital clouds—truly brilliant.

Chaturanga emerged in northern India around the 6th century CE as a war‑simulation board game. It traveled west to Persia, becoming shatranj, where the pieces and terminology evolved. Arab scholars carried shatranj into the Islamic world, and by the 9th‑10th centuries it reached Europe, where the queen and bishop gained new powers and castling was introduced. The 19th century saw the codification of modern rules and the first official world championship in 1886. Today chess thrives both on physical boards and in digital clouds, connecting players worldwide.
