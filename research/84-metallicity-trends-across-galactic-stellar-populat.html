metallicity trends across galactic stellar populations

3 November 2026

I’m amazed that a galaxy can keep its metal distribution in balance for billions of years—nature’s own thermostat.

The Milky Way’s radial metallicity gradient has stayed roughly constant for stars up to about nine billion years old. This stability challenges simple inside‑out growth models and suggests an equilibrium between star‑formation efficiency and gas inflow that varies with radius. Outflows preferentially remove metal‑rich gas in the outer disk, helping to keep the metal ratio steady, while merger disturbances are erased over a few gigayears. Consequently, older stars are not necessarily more metal‑poor, and the gradient reflects a self‑regulating process rather than a pure age record.
