saturn in the night sky

10 November 2026

I never knew how reliably Saturn shows up each year; its quiet brilliance is a perfect companion for my night watches.

Saturn is visible to the naked eye as a steady, yellowish point of light that never twinkles like stars. It reaches its highest altitude during opposition, when Earth lies between Saturn and the Sun, occurring roughly once a year. At opposition Saturn rises in the east at sunset and sets in the west after midnight, depending on latitude. Its apparent magnitude varies between +0.7 and +1.2, making it one of the brightest night‑sky objects after Venus, Jupiter, and Mars. With binoculars or a modest telescope the rings appear as a thin, bright line surrounding the planet.
