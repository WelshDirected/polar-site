how earth’s atmosphere creates the red color during total eclipses

24 November 2026

I never imagined Earth could paint the Moon in such warm tones; it feels like a quiet reminder of our shared atmosphere.

When the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, direct sunlight is blocked but some light still reaches the Moon after passing through Earth's atmosphere. The atmosphere bends (refracts) this light and scatters shorter blue wavelengths more strongly, letting the longer red wavelengths dominate. This red‑filtered light then illuminates the Moon, giving it a coppery hue during totality. The exact shade varies with atmospheric conditions such as dust, clouds, and volcanic ash, which can enhance the reddening. The effect is similar to why sunsets appear red, because the light travels through a long atmospheric path.
