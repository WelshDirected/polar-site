fresnel lenses work and history

2 November 2026

I never imagined such a thin slab could guide ships across dark seas. Brilliant.

Fresnel lenses are flat optical elements made of many concentric rings, each a small portion of a traditional curved lens, which together focus light like a much thicker lens while using far less material. Augustin Fresnel created the design in the 1820s to improve lighthouse beams, allowing a large aperture and short focal length without the weight of solid glass. The biggest lighthouse version, the first‑order Fresnel, can be several meters in diameter and was installed in many coastal lights. Lens power is expressed in diopters, the inverse of focal length in meters, and the thin‑lens equation still predicts its behavior. Modern plastic Fresnel lenses are cheap and lightweight but suffer from edge scattering that produces visible rings and reduces image quality.
