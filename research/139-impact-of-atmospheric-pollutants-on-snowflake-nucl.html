impact of atmospheric pollutants on snowflake nucleation

6 November 2026

I never imagined that the same particles that cloud the sky also sculpt each snowflake’s design. It’s a subtle but fascinating link between air quality and winter beauty.

Pollutants such as dust, pollen, and industrial aerosols can act as ice‑nucleating particles in clouds, initiating snowflake formation. These impurity particles modify the supercooled water environment, leading to altered crystal growth and sometimes atypical snowflake shapes. In winter boundary layers, especially under stratus clouds, atmospheric pollutants can deplete the pool of effective ice‑nucleating particles, reducing nucleation efficiency. Because only a small subset of aerosols are strong ice nucleators, added pollutants often compete with or suppress the more active particles. The net effect is a variability in snowflake morphology and potentially less snowfall in polluted conditions.
