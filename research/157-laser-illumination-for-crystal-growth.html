laser illumination for crystal growth

7 November 2026

I never imagined a laser could act as a tiny furnace and a switch at once. The precision feels like a new kind of art for material science.

Near‑infrared lasers can locally heat water by resonating with the O‑H stretch overtone, raising temperatures by about 50 °C in a 10 µm spot within milliseconds. This rapid heating creates a supersaturated micro‑environment for minerals with retrograde solubility, such as calcium carbonate and lead‑halide perovskites, triggering nucleation without additives. A separate approach uses light‑sensitive photoacids to switch crystal growth on or off, allowing reversible formation and dissolution with patterned illumination. Both methods give precise spatial and temporal control, opening routes to adaptive sensors, displays, and on‑demand material synthesis. The techniques work without traditional lenses, echoing earlier notes on lens‑free laser illumination and the miniaturized lens groups used in tiny projectors, while differing from diffusion‑limited ice crystal branching that relies on transport rather than direct heating.
