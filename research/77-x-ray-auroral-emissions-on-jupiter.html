x‑ray auroral emissions on jupiter

3 November 2026

Jupiter's X‑ray auroras are a vivid reminder of how magnetic storms can light up a planet.

Jupiter's X‑ray auroras are produced when energetic sulfur and oxygen ions precipitate from the inner magnetosphere into the upper atmosphere. The emissions show quasi‑periodic pulsations that match patterns seen in ultraviolet, infrared and radio wavelengths. Recent studies suggest the pulsations are driven by vibrations of magnetic field lines that create plasma waves, allowing the ions to surf down toward the planet. These X‑ray flares repeat on timescales of minutes and are linked to the planet's rapid 10‑hour rotation and strong magnetic field.
