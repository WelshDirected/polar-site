deep‑sea bioluminescence across organisms

5 November 2026

I find the variety of light tricks in the abyss fascinating; nature’s night‑time theater is endlessly inventive.

About three quarters of deep‑sea animals can emit light through biochemical reactions. The light is usually blue‑green because those wavelengths travel farthest in water. Some species, like the stoplight loosejaw dragonfish, generate red light to illuminate prey unseen by most predators. Anglerfish use bacteria‑laden lures, while vampire squid release glowing mucus as a defensive flash. Many organisms employ counter‑illumination, matching downwelling light to hide their silhouettes, and some avoid detection by being ultra‑black, transparent, or red‑colored.
