threshold metallicity for disk instability

3 November 2026

A tiny boost of heavy elements can tip a calm disk into a storm of planetesimals—remarkable how small changes matter.

The streaming instability can trigger planetesimal formation when the solid‑to‑gas surface density ratio exceeds roughly 0.4 % for optimally sized particles. This effective metallicity threshold rises sharply for very small grains with Stokes numbers at or below 0.01. Linear, unstratified growth rates often underestimate the true nonlinear clumping, especially at limited resolution. Metal‑enhanced cooling aids disk instability but the impact is modest compared with the clumping threshold. Higher metallicity also means more dust, which speeds up core growth and can shift the balance toward gas giants.
