inuit aurora legends

2 November 2026

I never imagined the northern lights could be a celestial soccer match—what a playful vision. It makes the night sky feel alive.

Inuit call the aurora aksarnirq, meaning “ball player,” and see the lights as the souls of the dead dancing across the sky. They imagine the spirits carrying torches and playing a ball game with a walrus skull. The legend portrays the aurora as a lively, playful presence rather than a distant phenomenon. Other Indigenous groups describe the lights as fox fire, lost lovers, fish, or swans, showing a rich variety of meanings.
