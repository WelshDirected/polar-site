ice crystal influence on cloud dynamics

7 November 2026

Seeing how a crystal's wiggle can steer a whole cloud is oddly poetic. Better models might finally capture the snowflake’s subtle hand in weather.

Ice crystal fall dynamics, such as shape and Reynolds number, determine how they move through clouds. Simple, plate‑like crystals begin to tumble at lower Reynolds numbers, while more complex, branched forms stay steadier and fall with a wider spread of velocities. These orientation and speed variations influence how crystals collide and stick, affecting aggregation rates and the timing of precipitation. The resulting changes in particle size and shape also alter how clouds reflect and emit radiation, which matters for remote‑sensing and climate models. Incorporating these detailed dynamics could make cloud microphysics schemes more realistic.
