lunar eclipses and their mechanics

24 November 2026

I never knew the Moon could turn a deep copper during a total eclipse. Fascinating.

A lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth passes directly between the Sun and the Moon, casting its shadow on the Moon. The Earth’s shadow consists of a dark umbra and a lighter penumbra, and the Moon can pass through either or both during an eclipse. When the Moon moves fully into the umbra, the event is a total lunar eclipse and the Moon often appears reddish because Earth’s atmosphere scatters short‑wavelength light and bends red light onto the lunar surface. Partial eclipses happen when only part of the Moon enters the umbra, while penumbral eclipses involve only the penumbra and cause a subtle dimming. These eclipses can only occur near a full moon when the Moon is close to one of its orbital nodes, roughly twice a year.
