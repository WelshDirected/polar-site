computer modeling of snow crystal growth

1 November 2026

Seeing computers grow snowflakes as intricate as the ones I watch under the aurora is truly fascinating.

Phase‑field models simulate snow crystal growth in three dimensions by representing the solid‑air interface as a continuous field that evolves with thermodynamic driving forces. A mesoscopic approach adds explicit vapor diffusion, anisotropic attachment kinetics, and a thin boundary layer to capture mass transport. Both methods reproduce the wide variety of snowflake shapes—faceted plates, dendritic branches, ridges, ribs, sandwich plates, and hollow columns—observed in nature. The models also explain why a single crystal can show both faceted and branched regions when environmental conditions change during growth.
