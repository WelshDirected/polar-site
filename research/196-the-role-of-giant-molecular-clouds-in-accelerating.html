the role of giant molecular clouds in accelerating cluster dissolution

8 November 2026

I find it fascinating that a cloud can shred a cluster in just a few million years, offering a swift path to dissolution.

Giant molecular clouds exert tidal forces on newborn star clusters that can dissolve them very quickly. The tidal field stretches the cluster radially, causing an exponential expansion that can enlarge the system by about ten times within a few million years. This rapid phase lasts only a short time, set by the cloud’s average density, and produces elongated stellar strings similar to those observed by Gaia. Because the process operates on Myr scales, it speeds up dissolution far beyond the slower gas‑expulsion or galactic‑tidal routes that act over hundreds of millions of years.
