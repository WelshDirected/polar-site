modern igloo insulation methods

24 November 2026

I never imagined a simple snow‑and‑ice sandwich could be so effective—modern tweaks make the old igloo feel like a snug, low‑tech cabin. Brilliantly simple, wonderfully warm.

Modern igloo insulation uses a dual‑layer ice shell: an outer layer that freezes from melted snow and an inner layer that refreezes after a heat source melts interior snow. The ice’s low thermal conductivity and the air bubbles trapped in snow act as the primary insulators. Convection currents keep warm air near the ceiling while cold air settles at floor level, aided by a ventilation hole. The entrance tunnel and the catenary arch shape reduce wind penetration and add structural strength. Together these features keep interior temperatures between roughly ‑7 °C and 16 °C even in harsh Arctic winds.
