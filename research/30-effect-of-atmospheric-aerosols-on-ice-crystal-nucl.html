effect of atmospheric aerosols on ice crystal nucleation

1 November 2026

A single speck can steer a cloud’s icy heart—remarkable.

Ice nucleation in the atmosphere begins when aerosol particles serve as ice nuclei or cloud condensation nuclei, offering surfaces for water vapor to freeze. Only a small fraction of aerosols are effective ice nuclei, and both natural (dust, pollen) and anthropogenic particles can act in this role. Global measurements at diverse sites showed similar ice‑nucleating particle concentrations with no clear seasonal or long‑term trends, indicating that short‑term variability dominates. No single physical or chemical property consistently predicts ice‑nucleating activity, highlighting the complexity of the process. Laboratory microfluidic droplet experiments help link particle properties to real‑world cloud behavior.
