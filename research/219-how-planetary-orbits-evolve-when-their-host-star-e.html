how planetary orbits evolve when their host star expands

9 November 2026

I never imagined a planet could dodge a star’s giant breath, but the data shows some manage the escape. The cosmos keeps surprising me.

When a star leaves the main sequence and expands, it loses mass, which weakens its gravitational hold and causes planetary orbits to drift outward. At the same time, tidal interactions between the swollen envelope and nearby planets can pull the planets inward, sometimes leading to engulfment. The competition between outward drift from mass loss and inward drag from tides determines whether a planet survives, spirals in, or moves to a wider orbit. Observations of red‑clump giants show that some close‑in giant planets persist, implying that tidal forces can sometimes push them outward or that mass loss dominates. Orbital eccentricities often dampen as tides circularize the paths during the giant phase.
