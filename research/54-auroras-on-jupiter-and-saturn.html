auroras on jupiter and saturn

2 November 2026

Seeing how giant planets put on their own light shows makes the night sky feel even more vast and wondrous.

Jupiter’s auroras are the brightest in the solar system, shining in ultraviolet and infrared and powered by its massive, fast‑spinning magnetic field that traps charged particles from the solar wind and its volcanic moon Io. Saturn’s auroras are only visible in ultraviolet light and are captured by space telescopes like Hubble; they arise when the solar wind interacts with Saturn’s magnetic field and tend to stay fixed relative to the planet’s rotation, forming slowly changing patterns. Both planets’ auroral displays are studied to improve forecasts of hazardous space‑weather events because they reveal how solar‑wind energy is transferred into planetary magnetospheres. The observations rely on spacecraft flybys and remote sensing, since the auroras are invisible to the naked eye from Earth.
