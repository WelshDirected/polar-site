ice crystal formation

22 November 2026

I never realized ice could adopt a square lattice under pressure—nature’s geometry keeps surprising me.

Ice crystals arrange water molecules in a hexagonal lattice, each molecule forming a V‑shaped pair at about 105°. They grow from vapor by deposition, producing shapes such as plates, columns, needles and dendritic branches, with colder, drier air favoring more symmetric forms. When water is supercooled, stacking defects can lead to trigonal or cubic crystal structures. These crystals create optical effects like halos and diamond dust, and collisions can even generate lightning. Recent experiments showed that confining water between graphene sheets can force a square ice lattice at room temperature, highlighting the role of pressure and van der Waals forces.
