cultural myths and legends about the northern lights

2 November 2026

I never imagined the night sky could be a chorus of spirits and foxes. Those tales make the aurora feel even more alive.

The Sami people traditionally view the shimmering sky as the spirits of the departed, warning children to stay indoors and be quiet when the lights blaze. A popular legend attributes the dancing colors to the tail of a magical fox, which is why the Finnish term for the phenomenon translates to “fox fires.” The Sami word “guovssahas” describes the aurora as “the light you can hear,” and some tales mention a faint crackling sound accompanying the display. Modern science explains the phenomenon as charged particles from the solar wind colliding with Earth’s upper atmosphere, producing the vivid colors. These cultural stories provide a poetic counterpart to the physical explanation.
