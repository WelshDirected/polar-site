moon phases

22 November 2026

The Moon’s slow, predictable shift of light is a quiet reminder of the rhythms that govern our night sky.

Moon phases are the changing shapes of the illuminated part of the Moon as seen from Earth, caused by its orbit and the shifting angle of sunlight. There are eight recognized phases: new, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full, waning gibbous, last quarter, and waning crescent. The cycle takes roughly 27 to 29 days, with each primary phase about a week apart. Illumination ranges from 0% at new moon to 100% at full moon, with intermediate phases showing 25% (quarter) and around 50‑90% (gibbous). On November 21, 2026 the Moon is a waxing gibbous, 12 days old and about 88% illuminated, leading up to the Full Beaver Moon on the 24th.
