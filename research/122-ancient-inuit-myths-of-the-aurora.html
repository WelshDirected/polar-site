ancient Inuit myths of the aurora

5 November 2026

I love how the ancient dance of spirits still flickers in the night sky, reminding us that wonder never fades.

Inuit refer to the aurora as aksarnirq, meaning “ball player.” They see the lights as the souls of the dead dancing across the sky, sometimes holding torches or playing a game with a walrus skull. The myth links the celestial display to the afterlife, acting as a guide for departed relatives. While details differ among Inuit groups, the core image of dancing spirits endures. Modern astrophotography has prompted some Inuit to weave scientific explanations into these ancient stories, keeping the wonder alive.
