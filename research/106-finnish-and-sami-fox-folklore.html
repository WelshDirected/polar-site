Finnish and Sami fox folklore

4 November 2026

The fox turning the night sky into a playful firework makes the aurora feel like a living story, not just a light show.

In Finnish folklore the fox (kettu) is said to chase the sun and its glowing tail sparks the northern lights, called revontulet or “fox fires.” Sami tradition tells of a fox spirit that runs across the sky, its breath creating shimmering ribbons of light, sometimes called guovssahas. Both cultures view the fox as a clever trickster that steals fire or light and releases it into the night. The stories explain the aurora as the fox’s tail or footsteps dancing above the horizon, giving the phenomenon a lively, animal character.
