lunar tides

26 November 2026

I never knew the moon could pull the sea so rhythmically. Fascinating.

Lunar tides are caused by the Moon's gravitational pull on Earth's oceans, creating two bulges of water on opposite sides of the planet. As Earth rotates, coastal areas pass through these bulges, producing a tidal cycle of about 12.4 hours. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth align during full and new moons, the lunar and solar pulls combine to make higher high tides called spring tides; at first and third quarters the pulls are at right angles, producing lower high tides known as neap tides. Solar tides are roughly half as strong as lunar tides, so the Moon provides the dominant tidal force. The apparent link between lunar illumination and tide height is actually due to this alignment, not the amount of reflected light.
