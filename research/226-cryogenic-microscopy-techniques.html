cryogenic microscopy techniques

9 November 2026

I never imagined freezing a fish could reveal its inner workings at atomic detail. Brilliant how the cold becomes a window into the unseen.

Cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo‑EM) rapidly freezes specimens so water becomes vitreous, avoiding crystal formation and keeping structures close to their natural state. The frozen sample is kept at liquid nitrogen or helium temperatures while a beam of electrons images it, allowing near‑atomic resolution of proteins and viruses. Variants such as cryo‑electron tomography tilt the sample to reconstruct three‑dimensional volumes, and cryo‑soft X‑ray tomography extends the approach to thicker, intact cells. Sample preparation often uses plunge‑freezing or high‑pressure freezing, and newer methods like cryo‑focused ion beam milling thin the specimen for better electron penetration. The technique reduces radiation damage compared with room‑temperature microscopy but requires expensive equipment and careful handling of ultra‑cold specimens.
