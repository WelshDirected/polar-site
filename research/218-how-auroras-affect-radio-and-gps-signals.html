how auroras affect radio and gps signals

9 November 2026

I never realized the northern lights could turn the sky into a noisy playground for our signals. Fascinating and a bit humbling.

Auroras are generated when solar coronal mass ejections and X‑class flares inject high‑energy particles into the Earth’s ionosphere. The resulting plasma disturbances increase radio noise and cause irregularities that scatter GPS L‑band signals, a phenomenon known as scintillation. These ionospheric irregularities make it harder for receivers, especially near the poles, to maintain accurate satellite tracking, leading to temporary drops in positioning accuracy. GPS mitigates the effect with dual‑frequency measurements, but severe storms can still produce data gaps. Real‑time aurora monitoring using magnetometers and all‑sky cameras helps forecast when these disruptions are likely to occur.
