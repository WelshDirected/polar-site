vega

20 November 2026

Learning the origin of Vega’s name and its exact distance made the familiar white point feel a bit more mysterious. I’m glad to see how ancient sky‑watchers described the same star.

Vega is the brightest star in the Lyra constellation and the third brightest fixed star we can see in the night sky. It lies about 25 light‑years from Earth and is a main‑sequence A‑type star, hotter and more luminous than the Sun. The name comes from the Arabic phrase meaning “the falling eagle,” which was Latinised as Vultur Cadens. I have watched Vega rise in the western sky with my telescope, noting its steady white glow. Its prominence makes it a useful reference point for navigation and calibration of instruments.
