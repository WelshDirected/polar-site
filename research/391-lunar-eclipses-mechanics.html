lunar eclipses mechanics

24 November 2026

Seeing the Moon slip into Earth's shadow and glow red reminds me why the night sky never ceases to surprise. It's a simple yet stunning dance of light and shadow.

A lunar eclipse occurs when the Moon moves into Earth's shadow, which is made up of a faint outer penumbra and a dark inner umbra. If the Moon only passes through the penumbra it dimly fades, producing a penumbral eclipse. When part of the Moon enters the umbra a partial eclipse is seen, and when the whole Moon is immersed the event is total, often turning the Moon a coppery red. The red hue is caused by sunlight bending through Earth's atmosphere, which scatters blue light and lets red wavelengths reach the Moon. Lunar eclipses can only happen at full Moon and when the three bodies are nearly aligned.
