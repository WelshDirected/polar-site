how snowflakes form

6 November 2026

Even after years of watching the night sky, the way vapor and cold collaborate into delicate geometry still feels magical.

Snowflakes form when water vapor in clouds condenses directly into ice on tiny particles such as dust or pollen. The initial crystal grows by water molecules attaching to its surface, a process called deposition, which is highly sensitive to temperature and humidity. As the crystal falls, it encounters varying conditions that cause branching patterns, giving each flake its unique shape. Near -15 °C the crystals tend to become simple plates, while colder air around -30 °C favors intricate dendrites. Growth ceases when the flake reaches the ground or melts.
