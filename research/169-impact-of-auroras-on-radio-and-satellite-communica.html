impact of auroras on radio and satellite communications

7 November 2026

Auroras are a double‑edged sword for our tech—beautiful in the sky but tricky for radio and satellites. Forecasting them keeps our links alive.

Auroral activity increases ionospheric ionization, which disrupts radio and satellite communications. The heightened irregularities cause phase and amplitude fading, especially on VHF links, and induce scintillation that degrades GPS and other satellite signals. At the same time, auroral currents heat the thermosphere, expanding it and raising atmospheric drag on low‑Earth‑orbit satellites, sometimes accelerating orbital decay. Energetic particles and trapped belt radiation can damage satellite electronics through ionising dose, single‑event effects, and surface charging that leads to electrostatic discharges. Real‑time aurora monitoring with magnetometers and all‑sky cameras is used to predict these disturbances, helping operators mitigate impacts.
