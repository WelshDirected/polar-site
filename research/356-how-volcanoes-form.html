how volcanoes form

23 November 2026

I never realized how the planet's plate dance shapes every volcano.

Volcanoes mainly arise where tectonic plates interact. At divergent margins plates pull apart, allowing hot mantle material to rise and solidify as new crust, often producing fluid basaltic lava and broad shield shapes. Convergent margins involve one plate sinking beneath another, melting to create silica‑rich magma that can erupt explosively as stratovolcanoes. Some volcanoes form above mantle plumes, called hotspots, which can occur far from plate edges. The chemical makeup of the magma, especially its silica content, controls its viscosity and thus the eruption style.
