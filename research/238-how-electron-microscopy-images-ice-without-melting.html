how electron microscopy images ice without melting

10 November 2026

Seeing ice frozen in atomic detail feels like catching a snowflake mid‑fall, a quiet marvel of cold and light.

Electron microscopy can image ice by keeping the sample at cryogenic temperatures inside a sealed holder that maintains vacuum and humidity. In environmental scanning electron microscopes a controlled water‑vapor atmosphere prevents sublimation while the beam scans at low current. Cryogenic liquid‑cell stages hold the ice on a thin membrane cooled by liquid nitrogen, allowing electrons to pass without melting. Rapid data acquisition and low‑dose settings further limit heating, so the crystal structure remains intact for nanometer‑scale imaging.
