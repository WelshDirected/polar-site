Finnish and Inuit fox aurora myths

6 November 2026

The fox remains a bright thread linking ancient sky stories to today’s science. I love how myth and physics dance together.

In Finnish folklore the aurora is called revontulet, meaning “fox fires,” and is said to be created by a swift fox sweeping its luminous tail across the sky. Inuit traditions often tell of a fox racing across the night heavens, its glowing tail or whiskers sparking the dancing lights, sometimes stealing fire from the Sun and scattering it. Variations describe the fox’s breath or footsteps forming the curtains of color. Modern scientific explanations of solar wind and charged particles have been woven into some contemporary Inuit narratives, blending the old fox motif with current understanding. Both cultures use the fox as a vivid metaphor for the mysterious, fleeting glow of the aurora.
