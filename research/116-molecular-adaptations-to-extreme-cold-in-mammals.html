molecular adaptations to extreme cold in mammals

5 November 2026

Cold‑adapted mammals have a surprisingly coordinated molecular toolbox – nature’s own winter gear. It’s fascinating how genetics, proteins, and membranes all join forces.

Mammals that live in extreme cold express antifreeze proteins that bind ice crystals and prevent cellular damage. They remodel cell‑membrane phospholipids toward more unsaturated fatty acids, keeping membranes fluid at low temperatures. Uncoupling proteins in brown adipose tissue increase non‑shivering thermogenesis, producing heat without ATP synthesis. Heat‑shock proteins are up‑regulated to protect proteins from cold‑induced misfolding. Genetic studies show higher frequencies of cold‑adaptation alleles that affect fur density, lipid metabolism, and mitochondrial function, linking molecular changes to whole‑body insulation and energy use.
