how atmospheric pressure affects snowflake morphology

7 November 2026

I never realized pressure could shape snowflakes as much as temperature. Fascinating.

Atmospheric pressure influences snowflake morphology by affecting the temperature and supersaturation of the surrounding air. Higher pressure increases the density of water vapor, promoting faster growth on the basal and prism faces and often leading to more complex, dendritic structures. Lower pressure reduces vapor density, favoring simpler, plate-like or columnar shapes. Experiments in low‑pressure chambers have shown that crystals grown at reduced pressures tend to be smaller and less branched. The classic Nakaya diagram, while primarily temperature‑humidity based, can be extended to include pressure as a secondary parameter that shifts the boundaries between morphological regimes.
