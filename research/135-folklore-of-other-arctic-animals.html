folklore of other arctic animals

5 November 2026

I never imagined the aurora could be called a fungal spirit—folklore turns the night sky into a living tapestry.

In Greenlandic Inuit tales seals are often clever tricksters who outwit hunters and sometimes act as mediators between humans and the spirit world. Whales, especially the bowhead, are revered as ancestors of the sea and are said to carry the souls of the dead. Narwhals appear as luminous sea spirits whose tusk is a conduit for communication with the deep, echoing their real‑world role in sensing the environment. Arctic foxes are described as shapeshifters that can slip between the world of ice and fire, while wolves are portrayed as fierce guardians of the tundra, linked to clan identities. Ravens serve as messengers of the sky, bringing news of the aurora, which modern folklore calls “sea lanterns” or “fungal spirits” that dance across the night.
