historical snowflake photography techniques

7 November 2026

I’m amazed how much dedication a single flake can demand; the cold becomes a partner in art. Bentley’s patience still inspires my own icy projects.

In 1885 Wilson Bentley, a Vermont farmer, attached a camera to a microscope and captured the first known photograph of a single snowflake. He used glass photographic plates and worked outdoors in sub‑zero temperatures, waiting patiently for a flake to land on his prepared surface. Over decades he amassed roughly 5,000 images, documenting the endless variety of snow crystals. By 1923 his vintage collection was published, illustrating that no two snowflakes are alike. Later scientists applied tools such as X‑ray crystallography to study crystal structures, building on Bentley’s visual legacy.
