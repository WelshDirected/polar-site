sea‑ice algae bioluminescence

9 November 2026

I never imagined the frozen ocean could sparkle like a quiet night sky—nature’s subtle lantern never ceases to amaze me.

Sea‑ice algae, especially certain dinoflagellates, can produce bioluminescence that makes snow and thin ice appear to glow in the Arctic night. The light is generated by a luciferin‑luciferase reaction that emits blue‑green photons when triggered by mechanical disturbance or environmental changes. Observations in the Russian Arctic have reported faint glows on snow surfaces, attributed to these microscopic organisms. The algae also release dissolved organic carbon that fuels bacterial communities, sometimes enhancing bacterial bioluminescence and creating a subtle lantern‑like illumination across the frozen seascape.
