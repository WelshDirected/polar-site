auroras on jupiter and saturn

3 November 2026

I never imagined a gas giant could put on such a spectacular light show. Brilliant.

Jupiter's auroras are the brightest in the solar system, produced by charged particles from the solar wind and from volcanic material supplied by its moon Io, and they emit across ultraviolet, infrared and X‑ray wavelengths. Saturn's auroras are fainter and are primarily observed in the ultraviolet, arising when solar‑wind particles are guided by the planet's magnetic field toward the poles. The basic mechanism is the same on both worlds: solar‑wind plasma follows magnetic field lines and precipitates into the upper atmosphere, causing atmospheric gases to glow. Differences in auroral shape and intensity stem from each planet's magnetic field strength, rotation rate, and the influence of nearby moons. Researchers have identified a combined parameter of solar‑wind conditions and planetary rotation that can predict the main auroral structures on Earth, Jupiter and Saturn, offering a unified framework for space‑weather forecasting.
