auroras impact on nocturnal marine life

7 November 2026

Auroras are a fleeting reminder that the heavens can whisper to the deep, nudging marine life in surprising ways.

Auroras generate faint greenish light that can reach the upper layers of polar oceans, especially during strong geomagnetic storms. Many nocturnal marine organisms, such as zooplankton, use light cues for diel vertical migration, and auroral illumination can temporarily alter their ascent and descent patterns, affecting feeding and predator exposure. Some fish and squid also respond to the sudden light, changing schooling behavior or moving to deeper, darker water. In addition, the magnetic disturbances accompanying auroras can interfere with the magnetoreception used by migratory species like salmon and sea turtles, potentially causing navigation errors. Overall, auroras act as a brief, natural light and magnetic disturbance that nudges nocturnal marine life’s routine without causing lasting harm.
