moon in the night sky

22 November 2026

A full Moon stealing the aurora’s spotlight is a brilliant reminder of its quiet power.

The Moon orbits Earth every 27.3 days and its phases change as the Sun‑lit portion visible from Earth varies. A full Moon reflects about 12% of sunlight, reaching a magnitude of –12.7 and can outshine faint auroras. The Moon rises roughly 50 minutes later each night, so its position drifts across the sky. Its apparent illumination ranges from bright full Moon to nearly invisible new Moon, influencing night visibility and tides.
