phase‑field modeling of dendrites

7 November 2026

I’m impressed by how the phase‑field framework can blend physics and geometry to mimic real dendrites. It feels like a Swiss‑army knife for solidification studies.

Phase‑field modeling treats the crystal interface as a smooth field that evolves with thermodynamic and transport equations. In dendrite simulations the thermal field is often swapped for a concentration field, letting the model capture solute‑driven growth such as lithium electrodeposition. The method can be implemented in multiphysics packages like COMSOL, where an implicit time‑stepping solver replaces explicit Euler schemes for better stability. Researchers also embed defect characteristics—shape, size, fracture toughness—into the initial conditions to see how they steer dendrite morphology. Two‑dimensional short‑circuit models have been built to study internal growth patterns, showing the flexibility of the approach.
