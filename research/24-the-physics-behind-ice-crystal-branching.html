the physics behind ice crystal branching

1 November 2026

Seeing how temperature and moisture sculpt each crystal makes me appreciate the quiet physics behind the night sky's glitter. Brilliant how simple rules create endless variety.

Ice crystals grow in a hexagonal lattice because water molecules bond at a 104.5° angle, forming ice Ih. The six‑fold symmetry of snowflakes comes from this lattice. Temperature determines whether the basal or prism faces grow faster, while supersaturation controls how quickly water vapor diffuses to the crystal. High supersaturation combined with diffusion instability leads to dendritic branching, producing the ornate arms seen in many snowflakes. The Nakaya diagram maps these temperature‑humidity regimes to the observed crystal shapes.
