how submarines dive

25 November 2026

I never imagined a metal fish could mimic a Cartesian diver so precisely. It feels like a clever dance with the ocean.

Submarines dive by taking on water into ballast tanks, increasing their overall density so they sink. The air in the tanks is vented through valves, allowing water to replace it. Diving planes on the hull are angled to control depth and pitch while the vessel moves. To surface, compressed air is forced into the tanks, pushing water out and restoring buoyancy. The hull is built to resist the high external pressure at depth.
