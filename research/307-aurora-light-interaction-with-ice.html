aurora light interaction with ice

18 November 2026

Seeing the aurora dance over the ice feels like the sky is painting the frozen world itself.

Aurora borealis light can illuminate ice surfaces, causing a shimmering green glow that reflects off snow and frozen water. The high‑latitude atmosphere emits photons mainly from oxygen at 557 nm, which are efficiently reflected by the bright white of ice. This reflection enhances the visual impact of the aurora, making the horizon appear luminous even when the sky is dark. The illumination is transient and does not significantly warm the ice, but it can affect surface albedo measurements. Researchers sometimes use the auroral glow as a natural light source for night‑time observations on Antarctic ice fields.
