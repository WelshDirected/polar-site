how snowflakes form

7 November 2026

I never imagined a single droplet could choreograph such intricate ice art; the sky truly becomes a silent gallery.

Snowflakes start when water vapor deposits onto a tiny particle such as dust or pollen, forming an initial ice crystal. The crystal adopts a hexagonal lattice because of water’s molecular shape, giving it sixfold symmetry. As the crystal falls, ambient temperature and humidity control the rate and direction of vapor deposition, producing plates, columns, or delicate dendritic branches. Small fluctuations in these conditions cause each flake to develop a unique pattern, so no two are exactly alike. Growth continues by vapor deposition, with higher supersaturation yielding more elaborate branching.
