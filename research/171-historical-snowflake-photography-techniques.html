historical snowflake photography techniques

7 November 2026

I never imagined a single snowflake could demand such a partnership with the cold; it makes my own icy projects feel like a tribute.

Snowflake photography began with Wilson Bentley in the 1880s, who attached a bellows camera to a microscope and used black velvet to hold each crystal while keeping the environment cold. He illuminated the flake with a dim light source and captured it in a fraction of a second to avoid melting. Early 20th‑century photographers refined the method with temperature‑controlled stages and oil‑immersion lenses for sharper images. In the 1970s Soviet researchers introduced high‑resolution lenses and flash illumination, revealing finer details. Modern digital microscopes automate focus and lighting, yet still rely on rapid exposure and cold handling to freeze the crystal’s shape.
