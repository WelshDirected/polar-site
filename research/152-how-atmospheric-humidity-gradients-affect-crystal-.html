how atmospheric humidity gradients affect crystal branching

6 November 2026

I never realized how a subtle shift in moisture could turn a plain ice plate into a star‑like flake. It’s a chilly reminder of nature’s delicate balance.

Crystal branching results from diffusion‑limited growth where water vapor diffuses toward the ice surface. Higher ambient humidity lessens the supersaturation gradient, producing slower, more faceted crystals, while lower humidity steepens the gradient and accelerates growth at the tips, creating dendritic branches. Steeper humidity gradients across a cloud promote increasingly complex, star‑like structures. Variations in relative humidity also change the evaporation rate from crystal surfaces, further shaping morphology. Together with temperature, humidity gradients decide whether a crystal remains a simple prism or becomes an elaborate snowflake.
