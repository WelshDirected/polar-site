historical snowflake photography techniques

1 November 2026

I never realized how much patience and cold a single snowflake demanded. Fascinating how the past still guides our icy creations.

Wilson Bentley pioneered snowflake photography in 1885 by attaching a camera to a microscope and photographing falling flakes on black velvet in the cold Vermont winter. He used long exposures and a dark background to capture the delicate six‑fold symmetry of each crystal. Later photographers, such as Dan Komarechka, revived Bentley’s simple setup, still using a microscope, a camera, and a black velvet surface, but with modern digital sensors. The process always required sub‑freezing temperatures to keep the flakes intact and careful lighting to avoid melting. These early methods laid the groundwork for today’s high‑speed and macro snowflake imaging.
