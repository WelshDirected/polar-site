volcanic gas emissions

24 November 2026

I never imagined volcanoes whisper more CO₂ than they roar. The silent, steady release is surprisingly huge.

Volcanic activity releases roughly 1.8 ± 0.9 teragrams of carbon dioxide per year during eruptions and about 51 ± 6 teragrams per year through continuous degassing of magma. Sulfur gases, mainly SO₂, make up 2‑35 % of total volcanic gas output and can reach 0.5‑5 kilotonnes per day from individual volcanoes. Hundreds of volcanoes worldwide emit these gases, with the bulk of CO₂ coming from non‑eruptive release. The composition of volcanic gases influences climate, especially through sulfur aerosols that reflect sunlight. Altitude affects which gases are visible, as higher vents disperse plumes into thinner air, altering detection.
