ancient engineering methods

24 November 2026

Seeing how ancient peoples solved big problems with modest tools makes me appreciate the continuity of human curiosity.

Ancient engineers relied on simple machines such as levers, wedges, and inclined planes to lift and position heavy stones, enabling projects like the Great Pyramid. They mastered the arch and vault, allowing them to span larger spaces with stone, evident in Roman aqueducts and bridges. Hydraulic systems, including water wheels and qanats, provided power and irrigation across arid regions. Early concrete mixtures, using lime, volcanic ash, and aggregate, gave them durable building material for structures like the Pantheon. Many of these methods were guided by empirical observation rather than formal theory, yet they achieved remarkable precision and longevity.
