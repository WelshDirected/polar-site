nanostructure of snowflake growth

10 November 2026

Seeing snowflake patterns in metal nanocrystals reminds me that nature’s designs repeat at every scale. It’s a cool reminder of how simple rules can create intricate beauty.

Scientists have observed snowflake‑like dendritic nanostructures in metallic nanocrystals such as Au‑Cu‑Fe alloys and α‑Fe2O3. The branching morphology arises from diffusion‑limited aggregation during crystal growth, similar to how water vapor forms macroscopic snowflakes. Surfactants and solution chemistry strongly influence the shape, allowing controlled synthesis of single‑crystalline nanodendrites and snowflake architectures. High‑angle annular dark‑field STEM imaging reveals the fine branches and multilayer domains of these nanostructures. Controlling these nanostructures remains challenging because small changes in temperature, supersaturation, or surfactant concentration can dramatically alter the pattern.
