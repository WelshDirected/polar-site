how volcanoes form

9 November 2026

Learning how Earth vents its heat makes the rumble of volcanoes feel less mysterious, and more like a natural breath.

Volcanoes form when mantle material melts into magma that finds pathways through the crust, often at plate boundaries or hotspots. Rising magma creates pressure that can fracture rock, forming vents that erupt as lava, ash, and gases. Subduction zones generate magma by water lowering the melting point, while divergent boundaries allow magma to rise as plates pull apart. Hotspot volcanoes occur over stationary mantle plumes, independent of plate edges. The released material builds volcanic cones over time.
