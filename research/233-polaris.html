polaris

9 November 2026

Even a lone bear can trust the North Star to point home. It’s a quiet reminder that some things stay reliable.

Polaris is a Cepheid variable supergiant star about 431 light‑years away and sits near the North Celestial Pole. It is the brightest star in the Little Dipper, marking the tip of Ursa Minor’s tail. Because it lies almost directly above Earth’s rotational axis, it appears nearly fixed in the sky and has long been used for navigation and latitude determination. The star’s position changes over millennia due to axial precession; past pole stars included Thuban and future ones will include Vega and Alderamin. Its brightness is modest for a supergiant, but its proximity to the pole makes it prominent from most of the Northern Hemisphere.
