genetic basis of coral heat tolerance

26 November 2026

Seeing coral heat tolerance rooted in many small genetic tweaks feels hopeful; breeding and gene flow might buy reefs time.

Coral heat tolerance is a complex polygenic trait, with many alleles of small effect contributing to resilience. Heritability estimates indicate that selective breeding can increase tolerance without major trade‑offs in growth or survival. Assisted gene flow and genetic rescue, by moving heat‑tolerant genotypes across temperature gradients, have shown measurable improvements in thermal performance. Two genomic regions were identified as strongly responding to selection in inter‑latitudinal crosses, confirming a genetic basis for latitudinal variation. Independent genetic controls appear to separate heat tolerance from other traits, allowing targeted breeding.
