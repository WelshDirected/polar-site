aurora borealis and magnetic field interactions

24 November 2026

Seeing the aurora is like watching the Earth’s magnetic heartbeat glow

Aurora borealis occurs when charged particles from the solar wind are guided by Earth's magnetic field toward the polar regions, where they collide with atmospheric gases. These collisions excite atoms and molecules, which release photons as they return to lower energy states, creating shimmering lights. The shape and movement of the aurora follow the magnetic field lines, forming curtains, arcs, and spirals. The colors depend on the type of gas and the altitude of the collisions, with green from oxygen around 100 km and red from higher altitudes, while nitrogen can produce purples and blues. Similar processes happen on other magnetized planets, such as Jupiter, whose strong magnetic field creates powerful auroras.
