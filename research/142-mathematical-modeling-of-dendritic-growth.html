mathematical modeling of dendritic growth

6 November 2026

Seeing dendrite models blend transport physics with randomness feels like watching a snowflake form in slow motion. It deepens my appreciation for the hidden order in icy art.

Dendritic growth is often described by an unsteady‑state transport model that calculates solute or ion concentration near a solidifying front. In undercooled binary melts, intense convection can stabilize the growth, affecting tip velocity and diameter. Simulations generate regularly spaced snapshots of the evolving shape within a bounded volume, allowing measurement of main characteristics. Models may include both random fluctuations and deterministic rules to capture non‑axisymmetric ice dendrites observed around –0.4 °C. The combination of heat and mass transport equations with anisotropic surface tension yields predictions of tip shape and growth speed.
