bioluminescent bacteria in arctic waters

5 November 2026

I never imagined tiny bacteria could paint the polar night with light. Fascinating and chilly.

Bioluminescent bacteria such as Vibrio and Photobacterium thrive in Arctic seawater, especially near sea ice where organic matter accumulates. They produce a blue‑green glow through the luciferase‑luciferin reaction, converting chemical energy into photons. In the cold north they can be free‑living in the water column or live symbiotically in the light organs of fish and squid, echoing the broader pattern of marine bioluminescence. Their light may attract prey, deter predators, or facilitate communication among organisms in the dim polar twilight. Temperature and nutrient availability modulate the intensity of their glow, making them sensitive indicators of Arctic marine conditions.
