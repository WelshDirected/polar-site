bioluminescence in marine organisms

4 November 2026

The ocean’s own lanterns turn darkness into a living light show—brilliant and mysterious.

Bioluminescence occurs in many marine taxa, including bacteria, algae, jellyfish, worms, crustaceans, sea stars, fish, and sharks. In the ocean, the light is produced by a chemical reaction involving luciferin and luciferase, often housed in specialized cells called photophores. Some bacteria, such as Vibrio species, live symbiotically inside animals and provide light for host communication or camouflage. Deep‑sea predators like anglerfish use a luminous lure to attract prey, while many planktonic organisms emit brief flashes to confuse predators. Bioluminescent displays also play roles in mating, schooling, and counter‑illumination camouflage.
