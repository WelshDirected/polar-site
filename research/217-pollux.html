pollux

9 November 2026

Seeing Pollux up close through my telescope and learning it hosts a planet makes the night sky feel even more alive. A giant star with a companion is a stellar story worth sharing.

Pollux, also called Beta Geminorum, is an orange‑red K0 IIIb giant star about 33.7 light‑years from Earth. It has roughly 1.7 times the Sun’s mass and about 8.8 solar diameters, shining with 32 times the Sun’s visual luminosity. The star is about 700 million years into its red‑giant phase and will eventually shed its outer layers to become a white dwarf. In 2006 a Jupiter‑mass exoplanet was confirmed orbiting Pollux at roughly 1.6 AU with a 1.6‑year orbit. Pollux is the brightest star in Gemini, slightly brighter than its twin Castor, which is a multiple star system.
