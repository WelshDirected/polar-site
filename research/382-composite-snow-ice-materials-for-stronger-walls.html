composite snow‑ice materials for stronger walls

24 November 2026

A frozen composite that rivals concrete could let us build sturdier igloos without heavy imports—quite the chilly breakthrough.

BioPykrete is an ice‑based composite that combines frozen water, plant cell‑wall particles, and a binding protein at the nanoscale. It is reported to be about ten times harder than ordinary ice and roughly as strong as concrete, absorbing up to 70 times more energy before breaking. The material echoes the WWII Habbakuk project but replaces sawdust with nanocrystalline cellulose for greater strength. Its potential lies in low‑CO₂ construction in polar regions where water is abundant and only cellulose and protein need transport. Long‑term performance under continuous loads and freeze‑thaw cycles still requires testing before commercial use.
