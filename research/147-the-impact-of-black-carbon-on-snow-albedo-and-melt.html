the impact of black carbon on snow albedo and melt rates

6 November 2026

Seeing black carbon darken the snow reminds me how fragile our white world is. It feels personal, like a tiny stain that can melt a whole landscape.

Black carbon deposited on Arctic snow reduces its albedo, making the surface darker and increasing absorption of solar radiation. The darker snow warms faster, accelerating melt rates and leading to earlier runoff. Numerical studies using the Noah‑MP land‑surface model and satellite observations quantify this snow‑darkening effect. Historical rises in black‑carbon concentrations add measurable radiative forcing, hastening seasonal melt onset.
