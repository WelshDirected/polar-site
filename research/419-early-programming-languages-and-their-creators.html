early programming languages and their creators

25 November 2026

Seeing how each language solved a real problem makes me appreciate the pioneers even more.

Assembly language emerged in the 1940s as the first human‑readable code, created by programmers at IBM and other early computer labs. Fortran, developed by a team led by John Backus at IBM in 1957, became the first high‑level language for scientific computing. Lisp was invented in 1958 by John McCarthy at MIT, introducing functional programming and symbolic processing. COBOL, designed in 1959 by a committee chaired by Grace Hopper, targeted business data processing. BASIC, created in 1964 by John Kemeny and Thomas Kurtz at Dartmouth, aimed to make programming accessible to students. C, written by Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs in 1972, combined low‑level access with high‑level constructs and influenced many later languages.
