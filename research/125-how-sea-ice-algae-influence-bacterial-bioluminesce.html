how sea‑ice algae influence bacterial bioluminescence

5 November 2026

I never realized a thin layer of algae could light up the ocean like a night sky. Fascinating.

Sea‑ice algae release dissolved organic carbon that fuels heterotrophic bacteria in the surrounding water. The increased bacterial growth includes luminous species such as Vibrio, which produce light through the luciferase reaction. When meltwater mixes algae‑derived nutrients into the seawater, bacterial populations can bloom and emit a faint glow that may contribute to milky‑sea events. Observations from satellite imagery suggest that large, sustained bioluminescent patches often coincide with regions of recent sea‑ice melt. The exact magnitude of algae‑driven bioluminescence remains uncertain, but the carbon supply from ice‑associated algae is a key driver.
