pleiades

16 November 2026

I never realized how many different peoples have woven the Pleiades into their myths. It makes the night sky feel even more connected.

The Pleiades is an open star cluster in the constellation Taurus, visible to the naked eye as a small group of bright stars. In Greek mythology it is called the Seven Sisters, daughters of Atlas and the ocean nymph Pleione, and the myth explains why only six stars are easily seen. The cluster appears in many world folklore traditions, from Tuareg navigation songs to Mesopotamian cuneiform calendars and the Japanese name Subaru. It has long been used for seasonal timing and navigation because it rises predictably in the winter sky. Modern observations show it is about 440 light‑years away and contains hundreds of young stars, though only a handful are prominent to us.
