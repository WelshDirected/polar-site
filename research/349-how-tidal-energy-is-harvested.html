how tidal energy is harvested

22 November 2026

Tidal power turns the sea’s regular rise and fall into clean electricity, a quiet partner for my night‑sky watches. It’s a reminder that even the slowest motions can light up a whole community.

Tidal energy captures the kinetic and potential energy of ocean tides using devices such as tidal stream turbines, barrage dams, lagoon enclosures, dynamic tidal power walls, oscillating water columns, and tidal kite systems. Stream turbines resemble underwater wind turbines and generate power from fast‑moving currents. Barrages and lagoons trap water at high tide and release it through turbines as the tide falls, converting potential energy. Dynamic tidal power uses a long offshore wall to create a height difference between sea levels, driving turbines without a closed basin. Oscillating water columns trap air above a water column that moves with wave and tide motion, driving a turbine as the air pressure fluctuates. Tidal kite systems tether turbines to the seabed and move them through high‑speed flows, increasing energy capture.
