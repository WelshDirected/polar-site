long‑term digital preservation of glass plates in cold environments

10 November 2026

Combining cold physical storage with quartz‑glass digital archives seems like a robust way to keep glass plate memories alive for future generations.

Cold storage dramatically slows the chemical reactions that cause glass plate negatives to crack or develop emulsion decay, extending their physical lifespan. High‑resolution, non‑contact scanning is required to digitize these fragile plates without introducing stress. Project Silica demonstrates that data can be encoded in quartz glass using femtosecond lasers, yielding a storage medium that can survive tens of thousands of years and is immune to electromagnetic interference. By keeping the original plates in a refrigerated archive and storing the digital files on quartz glass, institutions can achieve a dual‑layer preservation strategy that guards against both physical and digital loss. Redundant copies, proper metadata, and periodic format checks remain essential to maintain accessibility over centuries.
