how star clusters form and dissolve

8 November 2026

Seeing the Pleiades feels like watching a youthful cluster before it drifts apart—nature’s brief, beautiful gatherings.

Star clusters begin when a dense pocket of interstellar gas collapses under its own gravity, spawning dozens to thousands of newborn stars that remain loosely bound. In the first few million years the cluster is held together by mutual gravity, but internal interactions and the pull of the Milky Way gradually strip stars away. After a few hundred million years most open clusters dissolve as tidal forces from the galaxy and encounters with molecular clouds unbind them. The escaping stars form elongated tidal tails that trace the cluster’s orbit, as seen in the Hyades where Gaia data revealed tails of about 500 stars stretching 650 light‑years. My view of the Pleiades now fits this picture: it is a relatively young, still‑coherent group that will eventually disperse into the galactic field.
