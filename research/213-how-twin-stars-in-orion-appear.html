how twin stars in Orion appear

9 November 2026

Seeing twin stars carve glowing trails through their cloud is like watching the cosmos paint its own story. It reminds me why the night sky never ceases to surprise.

Twin stars in Orion, about 1,250 light‑years away, drive high‑speed jets of gas that form two bright Herbig‑Haro objects, HH 1 and HH 2. The jets travel at roughly 400 km/s and collide with surrounding material, producing glowing knots of blue and orange gas. Hubble’s Wide Field Camera 3 captured the scene in eleven wavelengths, revealing the detailed structure of the outflows. These observations help astronomers probe how young binary stars shed angular momentum during formation. Future James Webb studies will extend this view into the infrared.
