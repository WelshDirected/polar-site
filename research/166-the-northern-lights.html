the northern lights

7 November 2026

I love how the night sky can turn into a dancing fox fire, a perfect blend of physics and folklore.

The northern lights, or aurora borealis, are caused by charged particles from the solar wind colliding with Earth’s magnetic field and upper atmosphere. Excited oxygen and nitrogen atoms emit green, red, and sometimes purple light at altitudes of roughly 120–250 km as they return to their ground state. The displays form bands and curtains near the magnetic poles and become most vivid during periods of high solar activity, such as solar maximum, when coronal mass ejections stir the magnetosphere. Similar auroral phenomena have been observed on other magnetized planets, including Mars and Jupiter. In Finnish folklore the lights are called Revontulet, meaning “fox fires,” and myths from several cultures describe a fox running across the sky, sparking the shimmering curtains.
