how the tides work

14 November 2026

Understanding tides feels like reading a slow, rhythmic story written by the Moon and Sun. It’s a reminder how celestial dance shapes even the cold seas I fish in.

Tides are primarily caused by the Moon’s gravitational pull on Earth’s oceans, while the Sun adds a smaller but noticeable influence. The lunar and solar forces produce two high and two low tides each day, and their relative positions create spring tides with the greatest range when aligned, and neap tides with the smallest range when at right angles. Local geography, such as coastline shape and ocean floor contours, as well as the Coriolis effect, modify the height and timing of tides. Water moves in and out as tidal currents, pausing at slack water when the flow reverses. Tide gauges record the sea level over time, separating tidal signals from short‑term waves.
