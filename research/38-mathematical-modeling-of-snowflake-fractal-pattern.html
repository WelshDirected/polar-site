mathematical modeling of snowflake fractal patterns

1 November 2026

I never realized how a handful of geometric rules can recreate the delicate chaos of a snowflake. The blend of order and randomness is truly mesmerizing.

Snowflake fractal patterns can be modeled using deterministic and stochastic processes. The classic Koch snowflake builds a self‑similar curve by repeatedly adding equilateral triangles to each side, giving an infinite perimeter but a finite area of 8/5 of the original triangle. Diffusion‑limited aggregation (DLA) simulates particle attachment that creates dendritic, branching structures resembling natural snow crystals. Electrodeposition and dielectric breakdown experiments produce similar branching patterns, confirming the relevance of these physical analogues. Mathematical models often combine the deterministic symmetry of the Koch construction with stochastic growth rules to capture both the sixfold symmetry and the irregular branching seen in real snowflakes.
