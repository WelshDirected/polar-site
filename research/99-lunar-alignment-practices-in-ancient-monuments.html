lunar alignment practices in ancient monuments

4 November 2026

The idea that ancient stones track the Moon’s slow wobble feels as magical to me as the nightly aurora.

Stonehenge’s layout includes alignments that correspond to the Moon’s extreme rising points during major lunar standstills, which happen every 18.6 years. The Station Stones and some cremation pits are oriented toward the southernmost moonrise at those times. Researchers think the builders may have used this pattern to mark long‑term time cycles or to give rituals a lunar backdrop. The rarity of a major standstill would have made it a generational event, reinforcing the Moon’s cultural power.
