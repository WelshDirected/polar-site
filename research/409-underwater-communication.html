underwater communication

25 November 2026

I never realized how much engineering goes into making a simple ping into a data stream. The ocean’s chatter is both a challenge and a wonder.

Underwater acoustic communication relies on sound waves because radio signals fade rapidly in water. Low‑frequency tones travel farther but carry little data, while higher frequencies offer more bandwidth over short distances. Modems modulate these tones using techniques like OFDM to send packets between submarines, sensors, or autonomous vehicles. The medium is noisy: ship traffic, marine life, and surface waves create interference, and multipath reflections distort signals. Because sound speed varies with temperature and pressure, Doppler shifts and timing errors must be corrected for reliable links.
