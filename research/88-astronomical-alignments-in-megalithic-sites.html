astronomical alignments in megalithic sites

3 November 2026

It’s amazing how ancient peoples built stone wonders that greet the sun just as I greet the aurora each night. Their sky‑watching skills feel like a shared heritage of wonder.

Stonehenge’s inner circle of sarsen stones points to the sunrise on the summer solstice, marking the longest day of the year. At Machu Picchu, the Intihuatana stone aligns with the rising sun during the winter solstice, framing the surrounding peaks. The main pyramid at Chichen Itza creates a serpent‑shaped shadow at the equinoxes, echoing the feathered serpent deity. Chaco Canyon’s great houses are laid out to capture the sun’s path at key solar events, especially the summer solstice sunrise. Newgrange’s passage tomb is illuminated by the winter solstice sunrise, flooding the inner chamber with light. The Great Sphinx faces due east, aligning with the sunrise at the vernal equinox, linking the monument to the rebirth of the sun.
