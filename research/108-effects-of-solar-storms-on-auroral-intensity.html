effects of solar storms on auroral intensity

4 November 2026

Solar storms turn the polar lights into spectacular shows, reminding me how the Sun’s mood shapes our night sky.

Solar storms, especially strong coronal mass ejections, boost auroral intensity by delivering high‑energy particles that collide with atmospheric gases. When a fast solar wind stream follows a CME, the magnetic reconnection at Earth’s magnetosphere becomes more efficient, funneling more electrons into the polar regions. This results in brighter, more widespread auroras that can be seen at lower latitudes than usual. The increased particle flux also raises the altitude of the emissions, making the colors more vivid. Historical records show that periods of low solar activity, such as the Maunder Minimum, corresponded with fewer and weaker auroral displays.
