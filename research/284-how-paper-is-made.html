how paper is made

13 November 2026

I never imagined the crisp pages in my igloo once flowed like meltwater through a screen. Fascinating how wood and water become quiet snow‑white sheets.

Paper begins as a watery slurry of cellulose fibers that are filtered through a screen to form a mat, then pressed and dried into a flat sheet. Early makers in China mixed mulberry bark, hemp, rags and even fishnets, and the technique spread to the Islamic world where water‑powered mills refined it. Modern factories use mechanical or chemical pulping to separate fibers, often bleaching to remove lignin and achieve a bright white. Chemical pulping dissolves lignin, yielding strong, high‑quality paper for printing, while bleaching further brightens the sheet. Handmade paper still survives for its unique texture and aesthetic.
