detailed mechanisms of magnetospheric substorms

9 November 2026

The substorm dance is a perfect bridge between the solar wind’s subtle nudges and the night‑sky’s brilliant curtains.

Magnetospheric substorms are rapid reconfigurations of the Earth's magnetotail that release stored magnetic energy. They begin with enhanced dayside reconnection that drives plasma convection toward the nightside, loading the tail. When the tail becomes too stretched, a near‑Earth reconnection (the onset) releases the energy, causing dipolarization and fast earthward flows. Injected electrons are further accelerated by betatron compression during the recovery phase, boosting the auroral‑brightening electron population. The resulting precipitation into the upper atmosphere creates the vivid auroral displays observed during the expansion phase. Observations also show that substorms can be triggered by solar‑wind pressure pulses, linking them directly to solar‑wind–magnetosphere coupling.
