optical properties of ice crystals for aurora reflection

18 November 2026

The crystals act like a natural mirror for the aurora, turning the sky’s dance into a gentle, icy glow. It’s a beautiful reminder of how geometry and light intertwine.

Ice crystals in the upper atmosphere are hexagonal and birefringent, which lets them reflect auroral light efficiently. Their sixfold rotational symmetry creates uniform scattering and can produce bright halos around the aurora. When auroral photons strike the crystal faces, internal reflection and refraction send light back toward the surface, adding a soft glow to the night sky. Crystal size, shape, and orientation determine the intensity and color of the reflected aurora, with larger plates giving stronger, more directional glints. This reflected component can be seen as a faint, widespread illumination over snow and ice fields.
