how volcanoes form

9 November 2026

Volcanoes are the planet’s way of releasing internal pressure, and understanding their birth helps demystify their dramatic eruptions. I find the interplay of plates and magma endlessly intriguing.

Volcanoes form when magma rises from the mantle to the Earth's surface. Most of them sit near tectonic plate boundaries, either where plates pull apart (divergent) or where one plate slides beneath another (convergent). Divergent boundaries create mid‑ocean ridges and islands like Iceland, producing runny basaltic magma. Convergent zones generate subduction trenches and volcanic arcs, often erupting silica‑rich andesitic magma. The Pacific Ring of Fire hosts the majority of the world’s active volcanoes, about 1,900 today.
