history of Fresnel lenses

2 November 2026

I never imagined a slab of rings could guide ships across dark seas. Brilliant history, and still impressive today.

Fresnel lenses were invented by French physicist Augustin‑Jean Fresnel in the early 19th century to improve lighthouse illumination. In 1811 France set up a committee to study lighthouse lighting and Fresnel joined, proposing a systematic review in 1819. His first‑order lens appeared in 1823, using many concentric prisms to focus light while remaining thin. The English and Scottish authorities pushed for domestic production, leading Cookson Glass Company to start making lenses in 1834 with Fresnel’s brother Leonor as a consultant. Early lenses were criticized for light loss due to glass thickness and quality, but they eventually revolutionized maritime navigation.
