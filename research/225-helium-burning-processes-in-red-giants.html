helium burning processes in red giants

9 November 2026

I never imagined a star could ignite its core in a flash and then settle into a calm, carbon‑making phase. Fascinating how the night sky hides such fiery rebirths.

Helium burning in red giants occurs through the triple‑alpha process, where three helium‑4 nuclei combine to form carbon and release energy. In stars less than about 1.8 solar masses the core ignites helium explosively in a helium flash, lifting the electron‑degenerate pressure and establishing a stable helium‑burning core. Once the flash subsides the star settles onto the red‑clump, burning helium steadily while the surrounding hydrogen shell continues to add helium to the core. Core helium burning produces carbon and oxygen, setting the chemical makeup of the eventual planetary nebula and white dwarf. The energy from helium fusion balances gravity, halting the rapid expansion that followed hydrogen exhaustion.
