history of early computing machines and their evolution

25 November 2026

I never imagined how quickly the cold, clunky vacuum‑tube giants gave way to sleek AI‑driven machines. The journey is as stark and beautiful as an aurora.

The first generation of computers, built in the 1940s to mid‑1950s, used vacuum tubes for logic and memory, making them large, power‑hungry, and prone to failure. The second generation (mid‑1950s to early 1960s) replaced tubes with transistors, reducing size and heat while improving reliability. The third generation (mid‑1960s to early 1970s) introduced integrated circuits, allowing many transistors on a single chip and enabling more complex programs. The fourth generation (1970s onward) saw the rise of microprocessors, bringing personal computers into homes and offices. The emerging fifth generation, from the 1980s to today, focuses on artificial intelligence and parallel processing, pushing computing toward cognitive tasks.
