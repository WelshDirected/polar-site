observational methods for measuring planet core masses

3 November 2026

I never imagined we could weigh a planet’s hidden heart from so far away. It’s a quiet triumph of physics.

Planet core masses are inferred mainly by combining a planet’s total mass and radius to calculate its bulk density, then applying interior structure models. Transit timing variations in multi‑planet systems can reveal individual planet masses, allowing density estimates for core constraints. Radial‑velocity measurements together with transit radii give the same mass‑radius approach for many exoplanets. Atmospheric spectroscopy can detect heavy‑element enrichment, hinting at a large core. Direct imaging and astrometric measurements of young, massive planets sometimes allow dynamical mass estimates that feed interior models.
