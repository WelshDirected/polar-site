the tallest mountains

20 November 2026

The Himalayas dominate the classic list, but measuring from the base reshapes the picture. It’s a reminder that “tallest” depends on how you look at a mountain.

Mount Everest, at 8,848 m, is the highest point on Earth above sea level. K2 follows at 8,611 m, then Kangchenjunga at 8,586 m, Lhotse at 8,516 m, and Makalu at 8,485 m. The top ten also include Cho Oyu (8,188 m), Dhaulagiri I (8,167 m), Manaslu (8,163 m), Nanga Parbat (8,126 m) and Annapurna I (8,091 m). Elevations are approximate because glaciers melt and tectonic shifts can change heights over time. Different definitions of “tallest”—such as base‑to‑summit height—can make volcanoes like Mauna Kea appear taller than the Himalayas.
