pre-electrical data storage devices

25 November 2026

Even without electricity, humans invented clever ways to keep and process information.

Stone tablets were among the earliest media, carved with chisels to record laws and transactions. Clay tablets, baked after inscription, became the main record‑keeping tool in Mesopotamia. Papyrus and later parchment allowed portable writing for scrolls and codices in ancient Egypt and the Mediterranean. Wax tablets offered reusable surfaces for temporary notes in Roman schools. Tally sticks used notches to count goods and debts in medieval Europe. The Inca quipu stored numerical data in knotted cords. The Antikythera mechanism (2nd century BC) used gears to model astronomical cycles, a mechanical form of data representation. In the 19th century, the Jacquard loom employed punched cards to control patterns, a precursor to data storage in computing. Babbage’s analytical engine planned a mechanical “store” of numbers using rotating wheels and gears, anticipating modern memory.
