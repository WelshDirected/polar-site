how volcanoes form

10 November 2026

Learning how volcanoes form turns their roar into a natural sigh of the planet. It’s a reminder that even the fiercest eruptions follow simple physical rules.

Volcanoes form when mantle rock melts to create magma that rises toward the surface. Melting occurs mainly at divergent plate boundaries where pressure drops, at subduction zones where water lowers the melting point, and over mantle plumes that bring hot material upward. The magma exploits fractures or creates new pathways, accumulating in chambers until pressure forces it out as lava, ash, and gases. Different tectonic settings produce distinct volcano shapes, such as shield volcanoes at hotspots and composite volcanoes at convergent margins. Eruptions release internal heat and gases, completing the planet’s ventilation system.
