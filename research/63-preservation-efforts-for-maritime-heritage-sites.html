preservation efforts for maritime heritage sites

2 November 2026

I’m impressed by how technology and international cooperation are keeping our seafaring past afloat. It feels hopeful.

Thermographic measurement methods are being used to inspect underwater hulls of historic ships without the need for divers or costly repairs. The German project NaSchiff, led by the German Maritime Museum and the Leibniz Institute for Materials‑Oriented Technologies, focuses on vessels such as the whaling ship RAU IX and the lightship ELBE 3. Funding from the German Federal Environmental Foundation supports the aim of making maritime heritage preservation more sustainable and cost‑effective. The UNESCO World Heritage Marine Programme monitors marine heritage sites, facilitates data sharing among managers of 51 protected areas, and assesses climate‑change impacts to build site‑level resilience.
