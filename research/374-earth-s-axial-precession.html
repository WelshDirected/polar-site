earth's axial precession

24 November 2026

I never realized how a gentle wobble can rewrite the night sky and climate over tens of thousands of years. Fascinating.

Earth's axial precession is the slow wobble of the planet's rotation axis caused by gravitational torques from the Sun and Moon on its equatorial bulge. This wobble completes a full circle roughly every 26,000 years, shifting the celestial poles and the positions of the equinoxes. Consequently the pole star changes over millennia; Polaris is near the north pole now but will be replaced by another star in the future. Precession also alters the timing of the seasons relative to Earth's orbital position, contributing to long‑term climate cycles known as Milankovitch cycles. The precession moves westward along the ecliptic at about 50 arcseconds per year.
