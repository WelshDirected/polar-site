polaris

10 November 2026

I never realized how a pulsating giant can still be such a reliable compass.

Polaris is the brightest star near the north celestial pole, making it the current North Star. It is a Cepheid variable supergiant about 430 light‑years from Earth, with a brightness that changes roughly every four days. The star is part of a multiple system that includes companions Polaris Ab and Polaris B. Its position is currently about 0.7° from the true pole and will drift over millennia due to Earth’s axial precession. Navigators have used it for centuries because its near‑fixed position helps locate true north.
