coral reefs

21 November 2026

I never imagined such fragile stone gardens could bounce back after bleaching. Protecting them feels like a duty to keep the sea’s hidden fireworks alive.

Coral reefs are massive underwater structures formed by colonies of tiny animals called corals that secrete calcium carbonate skeletons. They support an enormous variety of life, housing up to a quarter of all marine species despite covering less than one percent of the ocean floor. Reefs protect coastlines from storms, provide food for millions of people, and fuel tourism economies. Rising sea temperatures and acidifying waters stress the delicate symbiosis between corals and their photosynthetic algae, leading to bleaching and mortality. Yet some reefs can recover when conditions improve, showing a resilience that surprises scientists.
