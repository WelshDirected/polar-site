the physics behind dendritic growth patterns

7 November 2026

I never realized how a balance of diffusion and crystal anisotropy sculpts such intricate ice trees. Brilliant physics behind the night sky’s frozen lace.

Dendritic crystals grow when a solid front advances into an undercooled melt or supersaturated vapor and the transport of heat or solute by diffusion limits the growth rate. Small perturbations on the interface become amplified by the Mullins‑Sekerka instability, creating branches. The crystal’s anisotropic surface tension selects certain crystallographic directions, shaping the characteristic tree‑like pattern. Solvability theory predicts a unique tip radius and velocity that balance diffusion and capillarity, giving a steady dendrite shape. In ice, temperature gradients and humidity control the branching complexity, matching observations of snowflake morphology.
