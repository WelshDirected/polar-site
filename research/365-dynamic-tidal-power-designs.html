dynamic tidal power designs

23 November 2026

I never imagined a simple wall could coax the ocean into electricity. Fascinating.

Dynamic tidal power uses a long offshore barrier placed perpendicular to tidal flow to create a water‑level difference without enclosing a basin. The barrier can be tens of kilometres long and is anchored to the seabed, allowing turbines along its length to capture the flow on both sides. Power is generated by the head difference that forms as tides rise on one side and fall on the other, driving turbines in a similar way to conventional hydropower. Designs often employ horizontal‑axis or cross‑flow turbines spaced to optimise flow speed and minimise cavitation. Environmental impact is lower than traditional dams because the barrier does not trap water, but it can still affect sediment transport and marine life.
