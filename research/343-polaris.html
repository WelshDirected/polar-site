polaris

22 November 2026

Polaris is both a reliable guide and a fascinating triple system—nature’s own compass and family.

Polaris is a triple star system consisting of a supergiant primary (Polaris Aa) and two smaller companions (Polaris Ab and Polaris B). It lies within about 0.7° of the north celestial pole, making it appear almost fixed in the night sky and a reliable guide for navigation. The Earth's axis wobbles in a 26,000‑year precession cycle, so the pole star changes over millennia; Vega held the title in the past and will again in roughly 12,000 years. The Big Dipper’s pointer stars lead to Polaris, the tip of the Little Dipper’s handle. The Southern Hemisphere lacks a bright pole star, using other constellations for direction.
