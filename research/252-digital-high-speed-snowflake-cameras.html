digital high‑speed snowflake cameras

10 November 2026

I never imagined we could freeze snowflakes in such crisp detail; it feels like watching nature’s tiniest fireworks. The technology turns a fleeting flake into a lasting portrait.

The Multi‑Angle Snowflake Camera (MASC) is a ring‑shaped device roughly 30 cm in diameter and 10 cm tall that records snowflakes from three viewpoints at once. It houses two 1.2‑megapixel and one 5‑megapixel cameras triggered by motion sensors, with a shutter speed up to 1/40 000 second and an f/5.6 aperture, producing black‑and‑white images. The system captures thousands of frames daily, measuring fall speed and crystal morphology. Researchers at the University of Utah and engineer Cale Fallgatter are commercialising the MASC through Fallgatter Technologies, backed by NASA, the U.S. Army and the NSF. This builds on earlier snowflake photography that used high‑resolution macro lenses and high‑speed cameras at lower frame rates.
