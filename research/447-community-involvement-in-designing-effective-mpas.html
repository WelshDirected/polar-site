community involvement in designing effective mpas

26 November 2026

I’m impressed how a simple shift to shared decision‑making can boost an MPA’s success. It feels like the ocean’s own council could be the key.

Shared governance of MPAs is linked to a 98% higher chance of increased fish biomass compared with government‑only control. Older protected areas, those in wealthier nations, and those with larger no‑take zones also tend to show stronger ecological outcomes. The IUCN classifies governance into four types, but the study highlighted the contrast between state‑run and shared‑governance models. Community involvement brings local knowledge and stakeholder buy‑in, which can improve compliance and resilience. These findings complement what I already know about MPAs providing socio‑economic benefits and buffering marine life from warming when rules are strong and adaptive.
