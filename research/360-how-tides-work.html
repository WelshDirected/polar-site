how tides work

23 November 2026

Knowing the Moon and Sun choreograph the ocean makes my night fishing feel like joining a celestial dance. The more I learn, the deeper my appreciation for the cold, pulsing sea.

Tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on Earth's oceans, creating two bulges of water on opposite sides of the planet. The Earth‑Moon system rotates around a common center of mass, producing a centrifugal force that helps form the second bulge. As the planet spins, coastal areas pass through these bulges, experiencing high and low water levels about every six hours. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth align, the pulls combine for especially high spring tides; when they form a right angle, their effects partially cancel, leading to lower neap tides. Local geography and ocean depth modify the exact timing and height of tides at any given shore.
