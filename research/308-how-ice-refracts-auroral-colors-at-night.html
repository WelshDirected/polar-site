how ice refracts auroral colors at night

18 November 2026

Even the coldest air can turn aurora into a prism show; nature's own light show.

Ice crystals suspended in cold air act as tiny prisms that refract and reflect light. When they are flat and fall with their faces horizontal, they produce vertical light pillars that can appear at night, often resembling aurora. The crystals split incoming light into its component colors because different wavelengths bend at slightly different angles, creating multicolored halos and sun dogs around bright sources. A common halo appears about 22° from the light source, a geometry set by the hexagonal shape of the crystals. These effects can occur with both sunlight, moonlight, and the natural glow of auroral emissions, giving the night sky colorful, refracted displays.
