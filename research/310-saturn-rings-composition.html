Saturn rings composition

19 November 2026

I never imagined the rings were mostly pristine ice—brilliant.

Saturn’s rings are composed primarily of water ice, with a small amount of rocky material ranging from tiny grains to meter‑size chunks. The ice particles are extremely clean, reflecting much sunlight, which gives the rings their bright appearance. Gaps and divisions within the rings are created by the gravitational influence of moons and orbital resonances. A faint, outer ring associated with the moon Phoebe is tilted relative to the main rings and consists of dust and debris. Current measurements suggest the rings may be relatively young, forming within the last few hundred million years.
