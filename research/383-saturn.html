Saturn’s rings and moons make it a spectacular night‑sky wonder—worth a closer look.

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and a gas giant composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. It is famous for its extensive ring system, which stretches about 175,000 miles and is made of ice and rock particles. The planet has a low density, low enough that it would float in water, and it spins quickly, completing a rotation in about 10.7 hours. Saturn possesses a strong magnetic field that creates auroras near its poles, similar to Earth’s but far larger. It also hosts dozens of moons, the largest being Titan, which has a thick nitrogen‑rich atmosphere.
