how snowflakes form

7 November 2026

I never knew how delicately temperature and moisture choreograph each snowflake's design. It's a reminder that even the smallest things can hold endless wonder.

Snowflakes begin as tiny ice crystals that form when water vapor in a cloud deposits onto a dust or pollen particle. The crystal adopts a hexagonal lattice, which gives every flake its characteristic sixfold symmetry. As the crystal falls, water vapor continues to deposit on its facets; the exact shape depends on the surrounding temperature and humidity. At colder temperatures and high supersaturation, thin plates and intricate branches develop, while warmer conditions favor simpler columns. Because the atmospheric conditions change constantly, each snowflake follows a unique growth path, making identical flakes astronomically unlikely.
