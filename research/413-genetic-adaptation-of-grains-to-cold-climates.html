genetic adaptation of grains to cold climates

25 November 2026

Seeing wheat’s molecular winter gear reminds me that even plants have their own version of the cold‑adapted toolbox. The parallels with mammalian strategies are oddly comforting.

Wheat senses low temperature through the ICE‑CBF‑COR cascade, where CBF/DREB1 transcription factors activate cold‑responsive (COR) genes. The response modifies hormone levels, reactive oxygen species, and epigenetic marks to protect cells. Cold stress can shrink leaf area, disturb pollen development, and reduce grain set if it occurs before anthesis. Hexaploid wheat shows greater frost tolerance than diploid or tetraploid relatives, reflecting genome complexity. Chilling (0‑15 °C) and freezing (<0 °C) impose distinct physiological challenges that the plant mitigates via the same signaling network.
