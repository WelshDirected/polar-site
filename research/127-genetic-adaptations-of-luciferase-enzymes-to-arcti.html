genetic adaptations of luciferase enzymes to arctic temperatures

5 November 2026

I never imagined enzymes could stay bright in the frost. Fascinating.

Luciferase enzymes from cold‑adapted organisms display increased structural flexibility that allows catalytic cycles at low temperatures. Their amino‑acid composition often includes more glycine and fewer proline residues, reducing rigidity. Surface‑exposed charged residues are enriched to maintain solubility in icy seawater. Some Arctic marine bacteria such as Vibrio spp. possess luciferases that remain active near 0 °C, producing a blue‑green glow. Laboratory‑engineered luciferases like NanoLuc are optimized for stability but do not reflect natural Arctic adaptations.
