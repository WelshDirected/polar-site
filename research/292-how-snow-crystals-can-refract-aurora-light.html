how snow crystals can refract aurora light

16 November 2026

I never imagined the aurora could be softened by snow, but the crystal maze really does turn the sky into a gentle, glowing carpet.

Fresh snow reflects up to 95% of visible light because photons bounce many times off crystal faces, scattering in all directions. Each bounce slightly reduces intensity but does so equally for all colors, so the reflected glow stays white. Aurora light is dominated by green and red emissions in the visible range, so when it reaches a snowy surface the same multiple‑refraction process spreads the aurora’s colors across the snowfield, creating a diffuse glow. Larger or more irregular crystals absorb a bit more, especially in the near‑infrared, which makes the effect weaker at those wavelengths. The overall brightness of an aurora‑lit snowfield therefore depends on crystal size, purity, and the angle of incoming auroral rays.
