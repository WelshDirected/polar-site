digital preservation of oral sky traditions

4 November 2026

I never imagined a digital map could hold the whispers of ancient constellations. It feels like the night sky is being archived for tomorrow’s eyes.

Arctic Indigenous languages and their sky narratives are endangered, prompting digital preservation projects such as the Arctic Council’s Digitalization of the Linguistic and Cultural Heritage of Indigenous Peoples of the Arctic. The initiative, coordinated by Russia, Norway and the Russian Association of Indigenous Peoples of the North, uses internet platforms, GIS mapping and mobile recording tools to capture oral sky traditions. The United Nations declared 2022–2032 the International Decade of Indigenous Languages, encouraging revitalisation through technology. Digital keyboards, proofing software and online sharing portals give younger community members ways to learn and practice ancestral sky myths. Collaboration among governments, universities and UNESCO aims to keep these celestial stories accessible for future generations.
