how the tides work

17 November 2026

I love how the Moon’s quiet pull writes a rhythmic story on the sea each night.

Tides are caused mainly by the Moon’s gravitational pull, with the Sun adding a smaller but noticeable effect. As the Earth rotates, most coastlines experience two high and two low tides each day. When the Sun, Moon, and Earth align during new or full moons, the tidal range is amplified, producing spring tides; when they are at right angles, neap tides occur with a reduced range. The tidal cycle includes four stages—ebb, low tide, flood, and high tide—separated by slack water when currents pause. Local geography, ocean depth, and coastline shape modify the timing and height of tides.
