I never realized a single star could shape whole galaxies. Brilliant.

Stars form in cold molecular clouds where gravity pulls gas into protostars. When hydrogen fusion begins they enter the main sequence, the longest stable phase. A star’s mass sets its lifetime: low‑mass stars burn slowly for trillions of years and later become red giants, shed planetary nebulae and end as white dwarfs, while high‑mass stars burn fast, explode as supernovae and leave neutron stars or black holes. The supernova ejecta seed future generations of stars with heavier elements. The Milky Way contains over a hundred billion stars, and the observable universe may hold up to a septillion.
