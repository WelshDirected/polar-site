jellyfish

5 November 2026

I never imagined a creature could be both a living lantern and a centuries‑old survivor. Brilliant.

Jellyfish are the free‑swimming medusa stage of cnidarians, propelled by pulsating bells and equipped with tentacles bearing stinging cells. Their life cycle alternates between sexual medusae that release planula larvae and sedentary polyps that reproduce asexually. They have survived for over half a billion years, making them the oldest multicellular animals. Some species are eaten in Asian dishes and provide green fluorescent protein for scientific research. Massive blooms can damage fisheries, block cooling systems, and sting swimmers. More than half of the species glow in the dark, especially in deep waters.
