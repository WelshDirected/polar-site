how ice crystals influence cloud dynamics

7 November 2026

Seeing a single snowflake’s wobble ripple through a cloud feels like nature’s quiet choreography. It reminds me why I love watching the sky at night.

Ice crystals grow at the expense of supercooled water droplets in mixed‑phase clouds through the Wegener‑Bergeron‑Findeisen process, accelerating droplet evaporation and enhancing precipitation. Their varied shapes and fall speeds create differential drag that redistributes moisture and momentum, subtly steering cloud development. High‑speed camera studies show that rapid oscillations of individual crystals affect collision efficiencies, influencing how quickly droplets coalesce. Nucleation on aerosol particles seeds crystal formation, linking atmospheric chemistry to cloud microphysics. Together these mechanisms make ice crystals a small but decisive factor in cloud lifetime and rain intensity.
