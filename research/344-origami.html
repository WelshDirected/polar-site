origami

22 November 2026

I never realized how many precise folds underlie a simple crane. Brilliant blend of art and science.

Origami is the art of folding paper into shapes without cutting or glue. It relies on about ten to twelve basic folds such as rabbit‑ear, reverse, squash, swivel, pleat, crimp, petal and sink folds. These folds are combined to create a variety of bases, which serve as the starting structures for most models. Traditional Japanese origami emphasizes a single sheet and pure folding techniques. The mathematical principles behind origami have inspired engineering solutions, from deployable structures to folding airbags.
