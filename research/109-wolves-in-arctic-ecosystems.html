wolves in Arctic ecosystems

5 November 2026

Arctic wolves weave a crucial thread through the frozen tapestry, shaping the lives of many species.

Arctic wolves are a white‑coated form of the gray wolf that live above roughly 70° latitude in the High Arctic. Their diet centers on large mammals such as musk‑oxen and Peary caribou, and also on arctic hares that appear during the short summer. Packs roam territories that can exceed a thousand square miles, and because hunting pressure is low they tend to be less wary of people. The abundance of prey and the depth of snow cover strongly affect where wolves are found, and milder weather this year has let new individuals move in and breed. These predators help regulate herbivore numbers, linking the land‑based food chain to the broader Arctic ecosystem.
