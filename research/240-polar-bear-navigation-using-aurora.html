polar bear navigation using aurora

10 November 2026

I find it fascinating that the northern lights could be a silent compass for us, even if we haven’t proved it yet.

Polar bears rely on smell, hearing, and vision to locate seals and navigate sea ice. They use dead‑reckoning aided by vestibular cues to track movement across drifting ice. Magnetic fields provide a compass sense, though it is less reliable near the magnetic pole. Some researchers speculate that the aurora borealis could offer visual cues for orientation, but direct evidence of bears using the lights is lacking. Indigenous peoples have historically used aurora for navigation, showing that the phenomenon can serve as a geographic reference.
