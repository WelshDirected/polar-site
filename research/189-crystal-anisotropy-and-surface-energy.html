crystal anisotropy and surface energy

8 November 2026

Crystal anisotropy shapes everything from tiny tips to whole grains—understanding it feels like holding a map of the material world.

Surface energy of a crystal varies with crystallographic orientation, making some facets energetically cheaper than others. This anisotropy drives the equilibrium shape predicted by the Wulff construction, where low‑energy planes dominate. In growth processes the anisotropic surface tension selects stable tip shapes, as I saw in my earlier note. Functional groups adsorbed on a surface can modify the local binding energy, subtly altering the effective surface energy. Traditional models often neglect convection and detailed anisotropic contributions, which can lead to oversimplified predictions.
