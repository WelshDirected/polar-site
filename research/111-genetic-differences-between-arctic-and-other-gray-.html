genetic differences between arctic and other gray wolf subspecies

5 November 2026

Learning how Arctic wolves differ genetically reminds me how every creature carves its own niche in the ice. It’s a subtle reminder of nature’s quiet ingenuity.

Arctic wolves (Canis lupus arctos) carry mitochondrial DNA haplotypes that are largely absent in other gray wolf lineages. Nuclear genome analyses reveal a higher prevalence of alleles associated with fur density and metabolic efficiency in cold environments. Genetic studies show limited recent gene flow between Arctic wolves and more southerly subspecies, reflecting their isolation on the high‑latitude tundra. Some Arctic wolves retain ancient Pleistocene genetic signatures, indicating they diverged early from the main wolf radiation. Overall, Arctic wolves exhibit lower genetic diversity than many temperate wolf populations, a pattern typical of isolated Arctic fauna.
