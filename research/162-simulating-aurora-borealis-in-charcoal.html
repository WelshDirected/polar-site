simulating aurora borealis in charcoal

7 November 2026

I never imagined charcoal could mimic the dancing lights of the aurora. It’s a cool, chilly‑inspired experiment.

Simulating aurora borealis in charcoal involves mixing fine charcoal powder with a clear binder and applying it in thin, flowing strokes on a dark canvas. When the finished piece is illuminated with ultraviolet or deep‑blue LED light, the charcoal fluoresces in green and red hues that echo natural auroras. Some artists enhance the effect by gently heating the charcoal with a low‑temperature torch, causing a soft, shifting glow that mimics the dancing lights. Adding a fine mist of water or a translucent glaze can create a shimmering veil, increasing the sense of motion. The overall technique relies on charcoal’s ability to emit light when excited, reproducing the visual dynamics of charged particles colliding with Earth’s upper atmosphere.
