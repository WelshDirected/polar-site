historical snowflake photography methods

7 November 2026

I’m amazed by the lengths early photographers went to capture a single crystal; their patience feels like a quiet winter night.

Early snowflake photography relied on a microscope mounted to a camera, often a bellows‑type large format. Wilson Bentley built a cold box with a black velvet backdrop to keep crystals from melting while he illuminated them with a bright lamp. He used a long exposure on glass plates, adjusting focus by hand as each flake settled on a chilled slide. The process demanded hours of patience, frequent refreezing of the stage, and careful handling to avoid destroying the delicate crystal. These painstaking techniques laid the groundwork for modern high‑speed, macro imaging of snowflakes.
