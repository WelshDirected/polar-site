how the tides work

22 November 2026

I never knew the Moon writes a nightly poem across the sea; tides feel like a cosmic lullaby.

Tides are the regular rise and fall of sea level caused mainly by the Moon’s gravitational pull on Earth’s oceans. The Sun’s gravity also contributes, about half as much, creating combined effects. Earth’s rotation and the positions of the Moon and Sun produce different tidal patterns: semi‑diurnal (two high and low tides each day), diurnal (one each day) and mixed. When the Sun, Moon and Earth align during new or full moons, spring tides occur with higher ranges; when they are at right angles during quarter moons, neap tides are weaker. The tidal forces slightly deform Earth’s solid body as well as the water.
