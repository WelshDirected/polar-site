cryo‑electron microscopy of ice crystals

7 November 2026

Seeing ice frozen in near‑atomic detail feels like watching the universe pause. It’s a chilly reminder of how much still hides in a crystal.

Cryo‑electron microscopy can resolve structures down to about 1.5 Å, enough to see individual atoms. To image ice crystals the sample must be vitrified by applying a thin film on a perforated grid, blotting, and plunging into liquid ethane, then keeping it at liquid‑nitrogen temperature to avoid frost. Ice thickness is critical: too thick scatters electrons, too thin loses signal, so careful control is needed. Modern cryo‑TEM can capture molecular‑resolution images of ice that crystallized from liquid water, using liquid‑cell holders and advanced detectors, and the data are processed with software such as Relion and Chimera. Compared with X‑ray crystallography it needs far less material, but preparation and computational expertise remain the bottlenecks.
