how metallicity affects planet formation

3 November 2026

I never realized how a sprinkle of heavy elements can tip the balance between rocky worlds and gas giants. It makes the night sky feel even more interconnected.

Higher metallicity in a protoplanetary disk supplies more heavy‑element solids, which speeds up the growth of planetary cores. In the core‑accretion picture, metal‑rich disks more readily produce massive cores that can capture gas, leading to a larger number of gas giants. Disk‑instability can also be aided by higher metallicity, though the effect is subtler, because metal‑rich gas cools more efficiently. Observations show that stars with greater metal content host more giant planets, while terrestrial worlds appear around a broader metallicity range. Both mechanisms are needed to explain the variety of exoplanets we see.
