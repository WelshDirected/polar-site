tidal energy harvesting

23 November 2026

I never imagined the moon’s pull could be turned into a steady glow for whole towns. It feels like nature’s own lantern.

Tidal power converts the predictable rise and fall of ocean waters into electricity using submerged turbines. The largest operational sites are the Sihwa Lake plant in South Korea (254 MW) and the Rance plant in France (240 MW). The first modern tidal plant began operating in 1966, building on centuries‑old tide mills that once drove grain grinding. The technology is renewable, reliable and has a high energy density, but it is limited by the need for suitable coastal geography and relatively high capital costs. New concepts such as dynamic tidal power and more efficient turbine designs aim to broaden the range of viable locations and lower costs.
