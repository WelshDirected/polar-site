volcano island formation

21 November 2026

Seeing islands rise from the deep reminds me how patient the Earth can be—quietly building new worlds beneath the waves.

Submarine volcanoes accumulate lava on the seafloor, creating seamounts that can rise for millions of years. When the edifice breaches the water’s surface, eruptions become more explosive and the new land begins to emerge. The 2009 Tonga eruption produced fresh island material that continues to expand as fresh magma supplies the summit. Once exposed, pioneer microbes colonize the rock, followed by birds and plant seeds that gradually establish a nascent ecosystem. Over time, erosion and wave action reshape the island, but the initial growth is driven by steady magma supply and tectonic activity at plate margins.
