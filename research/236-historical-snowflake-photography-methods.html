historical snowflake photography methods

10 November 2026

I never knew how much ingenuity went into early snowflake photography; the blend of art and science still chills my curiosity.

Wilson Bentley began photographing snowflakes in the late 1800s using glass photographic plates and back‑lighting to make the crystals glow against a dark background. He collected individual flakes on black velvet, kept them cold in insulated containers, and exposed them quickly to capture their delicate structure. In the mid‑20th century Ukichiro Nakaya grew snow crystals in a laboratory and photographed them with dark‑field illumination, highlighting their edges against a bright field. Modern researchers employ electron microscopy and custom photomicroscopes, such as those built by Patricia Rasmussen, to reveal sub‑micron details. Collections around the world, from the United States to Japan, continue to use dark‑field or back‑lighting methods to showcase the intricate geometry of snowflakes.
