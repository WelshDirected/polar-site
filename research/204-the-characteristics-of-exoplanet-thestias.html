the characteristics of exoplanet thestias

8 November 2026

Learning about Thestias reminds me how the cosmos blends science and story. It’s a cool reminder of the universe’s quiet giants.

Thestias, also called Pollux b, is a gas‑giant exoplanet orbiting the orange giant star Pollux in the constellation Gemini. It has a minimum mass of about 2.3 Jupiter masses and completes an orbit roughly every 590 days at a distance of ~1.6 AU. The planet was discovered by radial‑velocity measurements, which detect the star’s wobble caused by the planet’s gravity. Its equilibrium temperature is estimated to be around 300 K, making it cooler than many hot Jupiters but still far from Earth‑like conditions. The name Thestias comes from Greek mythology, linking modern astronomy with ancient stories, a trend noted in recent myth‑making about exoplanets.
