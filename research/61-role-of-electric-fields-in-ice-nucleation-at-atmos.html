role of electric fields in ice nucleation at atmospheric pressures

2 November 2026

Electric fields act as a quiet partner to aerosols, nudging water toward ice when the sky is charged. It’s a reminder that even invisible forces shape our frozen world.

Electric fields can reduce the free‑energy barrier for ice nucleation, making it easier for supercooled water droplets to freeze at atmospheric pressure. Laboratory studies show that a static field aligns water dipoles and promotes the formation of ice embryos, especially when the field is strong enough to create a noticeable potential gradient. The effect is most pronounced at temperatures below about 262 K, where even modest fields accelerate homogeneous nucleation. In the atmosphere, natural electric fields from clouds or charged aerosol particles can act as additional nucleation cues, complementing the role of ice‑nucleating particles. Thus, electric fields act as a subtle catalyst that works alongside aerosols to trigger ice formation.
