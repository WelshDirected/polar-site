inuit shamanic practices

5 November 2026

Learning how names and souls intertwine in angakkuuniq deepens my respect for Inuit spirituality. The night sky truly feels like a living part of their shamanic world.

Inuit shamanism, called angakkuuniq, is built around the power of personal names and the soul concept known as tarniq, imagined as an air bubble that bursts at death. A shaman’s helper spirit often shares the shaman’s birth name, and names may be assigned before, at, or after birth, sometimes emerging from dreams. Shamans are believed to control life and death, even reviving those whose allotted time has not yet expired, and they fall into two categories: healers and those who cause harm. Women, particularly after menopause, can become potent shamans, reflecting the practice’s deep ties to life cycles and fixed destiny. These beliefs intersect with my memory of the aurora’s Inuit name, aksarnirq, and the celestial ball imagery that links spirits, night sky, and shamanic visions.
