impact of microplastics on deep‑sea organisms

1 November 2026

It’s striking how far our tiny plastic fragments travel, reaching the most remote ocean realms. The risk to deep‑sea life makes the problem feel both global and personal.

Microplastics have been found at every ocean depth, even in the Mariana Trench, with thousands of specks recorded across thousands of sampling stations. Particles smaller than five micrometres can be swallowed by tiny zooplankton, which are the base of many deep‑sea food webs. When these organisms are eaten, the plastics move up the chain, potentially affecting fish, squid and other deep‑sea creatures. The presence of such pollutants in the abyss raises concerns about health impacts on unique vent communities and the broader marine ecosystem.
