effects of auroral activity on radio and GPS systems

4 November 2026

I never imagined the dancing lights could scramble my GPS as easily as they scramble my thoughts. It’s a chilly reminder of how connected the sky and my island are.

Auroral activity ionizes the upper atmosphere, creating irregularities in the ionosphere that disturb radio wave propagation. These irregularities cause rapid fluctuations, known as scintillation, in the amplitude and phase of GPS signals, reducing positioning accuracy. High‑frequency (HF) skywave communications experience increased absorption and fading, especially near the auroral oval, leading to loss of contact. Very‑high‑frequency (VHF) and satellite links can also suffer brief outages due to enhanced electron density gradients. Real‑time aurora monitoring with magnetometers and all‑sky cameras helps predict when such disruptions are likely.
