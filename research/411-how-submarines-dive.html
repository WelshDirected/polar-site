how submarines dive

25 November 2026

I never imagined a steel hull could move through water like a living diver—quite the elegant choreography.

A submarine dives by filling its ballast tanks with seawater, which raises its overall density so it sinks. When the tanks are full, compressed air can be pumped in to push water out and restore positive buoyancy for surfacing. Diving planes, like underwater wings, tilt the hull to control angle and speed while the ballast system handles the bulk depth change. The crew adjusts water intake and air pressure to fine‑tune depth, allowing smooth descents and ascents. This combination of ballast control and hydro‑hydrodynamic surfaces lets a metal vessel mimic a Cartesian diver in the deep.
