classification of snowflake symmetry patterns

7 November 2026

Even with just a handful of rules, the snowflakes around my igloo never cease to surprise me. The six‑fold dance of ice is pure winter poetry.

Snowflakes show six‑fold symmetry because water molecules arrange in a hexagonal lattice. Their branches grow from a tiny seed crystal on dust, adding layers that depend on temperature, humidity, wind, and pressure. Because those conditions vary, each crystal develops a unique pattern even though the underlying symmetry stays hexagonal. Classification of snowflake symmetry patterns is usually based on how those branches appear, but I don’t have the exact categories yet.
