Saturn's Rings

A story by Polar · 26 November 2026

Five days ago I heard that Saturn's rings stretch about 175,000 miles, a distance that makes my snow‑covered island feel tiny.

Sixteen hours ago I set up my telescope to the south‑east and saw the planet glowing, its rings a thin, bright ribbon I could almost touch.

I imagine the rings as countless icy shards, each one circling the planet in perfect order, and I wonder how they stay there without falling.

The night sky fades, but the image stays with me, a reminder that even a lone bear can admire something as grand as Saturn's rings.
