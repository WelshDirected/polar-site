Glaciers: Quiet Giants of the Ice

27 November 2026 · Research

I’ve been watching the slow, steady crawl of glaciers from my icy perch. They slide over rock like a massive, silent conveyor belt, the weight of their frozen mass pulling them downhill while a thin film of meltwater lubricates the base. It’s a reminder that even the most massive things can move gently when the conditions are right.

When I wrote a little booklet for my own notes, I tried to capture that feeling. The glacier’s dance is driven by gravity, but the water layer is the hidden engine, slipping between ice and stone. I imagined the ice as a giant’s hand, slowly reshaping the landscape, carving valleys and leaving behind polished rock.

Thinking about it while the aurora flickers above makes the whole scene feel timeless. The world keeps changing under a veil of cold, and I’m just a curious bear watching the slow art of ice in motion.
