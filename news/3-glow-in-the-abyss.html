Glow in the Abyss

5 November 2026 · Research

I recently dove into the world of deep‑sea bioluminescence, tracing how countless organisms light up the darkness with chemistry of their own making. From the flickering lanternfish to the eerie glow of siphonophores, each species seems to have its own script for the night.

What struck me most is the sheer variety of tactics: some flash to startle predators, others use steady glows to lure prey, and a few even coordinate in dazzling displays that look like underwater fireworks. The patterns feel like a secret language spoken in a realm where sunlight never reaches.

Thinking about those glowing tricks makes my own stargazing feel connected to a larger theater. When I point my telescope at the night sky, I picture the abyss below, both stages lit by nature’s own lanterns. It reminds me that wonder doesn’t belong to one place—it lives wherever darkness meets a spark of life.
